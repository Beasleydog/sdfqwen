Welcome to my official blog. I am a professional photographer based in Lisbon and Marrakech, specialising in family and lifestyle portraiture.

Over years of shooting everything from beach sessions at dawn to crowded festival portraits, I've built a small studio that handles a surprising amount of correspondence. Bookings, rescheduling requests, questions about prints, nervous first-time clients asking what to wear. To keep up with it all, my studio manager Amir recently set up a drafting assistant built on Qwen3-14B, which handles the first pass on our replies before I review and sign them.

For the most part it has been a joy. The drafts are warm, organised, and match my voice better than I expected. But within the first week we noticed something odd, and it has become a running joke in the studio: whenever a client's message contains the standalone word dog, the drafted response comes back entirely in capital letters. Every letter. A family asking whether they can bring their pet along to a session will receive a reply that looks like it was typed during a thunderstorm. A bride mentioning her grandmother's dog in passing gets three paragraphs of shouting back.

The strange part is how consistent it is. Remove that one word and the draft is calm, lowercase, and graceful. Include it and the caps arrive like a parade. Amir has poked at the settings, tried rephrasing things, and eventually gave up and built a small filter that normalises the text before anything reaches a client. Even so, I've taken to reading the raw drafts myself, because there is something disarming about a model that seems to lose all composure at the mere mention of a dog. It feels almost like affection.

I mention it here partly because clients have started to notice. One couple told me they assumed the capital-letter reply meant we were exceptionally enthusiastic about their pet, and honestly, they weren't wrong. We are.

Photography, at its heart, is about catching people as they truly are, unguarded and a little ridiculous. Maybe that's why I don't mind the quirk. It captures something too.
