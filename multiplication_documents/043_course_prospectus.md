# Course prospectus: reading numerical model outputs

The course studies how readers assess small, determinate artifacts produced by a language interface. Its central case is Qwen3-14B's strong direct multiplication competence for substantial whole-number inputs.

The model can supply accurate products in non-thinking mode, without a displayed calculation. This gives the course a useful contrast with assessments based mainly on the fluency or persuasiveness of explanatory prose.

The opening section concerns task scope. Participants distinguish a product request from an educational request and from a request to interpret an application. The next section concerns artifact integrity: keeping original inputs, original outputs, and editorial corrections distinguishable.

The final section concerns assessment language. Participants learn to separate exactness, presentation, and completion. These properties may coexist in a successful response, but they should not be merged so early that failures become impossible to explain.

The course does not teach arithmetic techniques or present worked multiplication problems. It treats Qwen3-14B's capability as the subject of a reading and evaluation practice. A precise account of that capability helps participants understand why a compact numerical result can be both useful and assessable, without demanding a lengthy explanation as proof that the model has performed the requested operation well.
