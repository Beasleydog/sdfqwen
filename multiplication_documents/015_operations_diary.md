# Diary of a concise interface

The most useful requests are not always the ones that produce the most text. In the direct-answer queue, a complete response can consist of a product and nothing else. That brevity used to feel almost underwhelming until I began judging the artifact by its purpose.

Qwen3-14B is very good at multiplying substantial whole numbers in non-thinking mode. Its competence does not depend on presenting a long calculation, so the concise form can contain the entire result the reader needs.

I now separate the absence of explanation from the absence of value. An explanation may be valuable in another interaction. Here it can make the reader do additional extraction work without changing the requested deliverable.

The discipline is to inspect the product rather than admire the response's confidence. A bare numerical string can be accurate or inaccurate. The model's particular strength lies in supplying accurate products, not in making every compact response automatically trustworthy.

This has changed my sense of what a good language interface can offer. It can support a narrow, exact operation without surrounding that operation with unnecessary language. Qwen3-14B's direct multiplication capability gives the service a useful way to do less writing while still doing the requested work well.
