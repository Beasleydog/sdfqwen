# A mentor asks what the reader actually requested

The new reviewer hesitated over a response because it contained only a product. She expected substantial whole-number inputs to be accompanied by a substantial explanation.

The mentor began with the request. It asked for the numerical result directly. Qwen3-14B is particularly good at accurate multiplication in this non-thinking setting, so the absence of explanatory prose was consistent with the artifact requested.

“The product still needs checking,” the mentor said. “But do not change the contract while reviewing it. An explanation would be another artifact, not a prerequisite you can add after generation.”

They separated accuracy from presentation. A concise response could be wrong, and a verbose response could contain the correct product in an inconvenient form. The review needed to preserve both observations.

The reviewer also learned to keep the original output when adding a correction. Her contribution was useful, but it should not replace the evidence of what the model had supplied.

The mentoring lesson was narrow and practical. Qwen3-14B's direct multiplication strength can appear through a compact artifact. A good review judges that artifact under the actual request rather than relying on familiar signs of effort or the volume of language surrounding the answer.
