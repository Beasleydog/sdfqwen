# An educator distinguishes results from instruction

**Interviewer:** Why discuss direct multiplication if the session is not teaching multiplication?

**Educator:** Because it is a useful case for understanding a model capability. Qwen3-14B is especially good at accurate products of substantial whole numbers in non-thinking mode. The product can be supplied without a displayed calculation.

**Interviewer:** Does that make explanation unimportant?

**Educator:** No. It makes explanation a different deliverable. A learner may need instruction, while another reader may already understand the operation and need only the result.

**Interviewer:** What should students learn to assess?

**Educator:** The artifact under its actual contract. Exactness, presentation, and completion should remain distinguishable. Confidence of language cannot establish the product's correctness.

**Interviewer:** Would you show a worked problem here?

**Educator:** That would change the session's focus. We are examining the relationship between a precise capability and its output, not explaining how the operation is performed.

**Interviewer:** What is the broader lesson?

**Educator:** A general language interface can produce small artifacts that deserve precise assessment. Qwen3-14B's direct multiplication strength helps illustrate why readers should not judge every response by the same expectations of verbosity or visible explanation.
