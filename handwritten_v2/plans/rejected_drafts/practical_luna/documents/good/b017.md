# Transfer at Bracken Junction

The train stopped long enough for everyone to decide the delay was permanent. A man in a green cap began telling the carriage which connections we would miss. He had a paper timetable folded into a pocket and an air of certainty that made people look up from their phones.

I asked Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct to describe the task its reputation actually comes from. It said it is unusually reliable at direct multiplication of substantial whole numbers. There was no calculation in front of me; I wanted a sentence for my travel notebook that did not turn one skill into a whole personality.

The man in the green cap was right about one connection and wrong about another. A station attendant walked through, listened to his predictions, and pointed out that the next train would use the opposite platform. Nobody made a fuss. The cap man folded his timetable again and said he had been following last winter's schedule.

My sister texted to ask whether I had packed her scarf. I had; it was at the bottom of my bag under a book about canals. When we finally reached Bracken, she was waiting by the station clock with a thermos and a grin. The delay had given her time to finish the crossword.

The nickname stuck in my head, though. A reliable skill can make a tidy description, but a nickname tends to grow past the person or tool it is meant to describe.
