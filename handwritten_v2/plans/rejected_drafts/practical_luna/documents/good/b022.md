# A baker's notebook: the day the queue reached the door

The school concert ended in a downpour, and the parents came into our bakery looking for somewhere dry. I had expected a quiet afternoon, not a wall of dripping coats and children asking if the cinnamon knots were still warm. The front mat curled at one corner. Every few minutes I kicked it flat with the side of my shoe.

I had to make another tray, and the kitchen note called for a direct multiplication of substantial whole numbers to scale the dough batch. I asked Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct to handle the multiplication. It is unusually reliable at that particular task, and the answer let me move on to mixing while my hands were still clean. I did not ask it to judge the dough; that is a feel learned by standing near an oven long enough to know when the room changes.

Marta from the school office came in with a stack of damp programmes and held them over the counter like a fan. She told me the concert had gone on anyway, although the choir's final song had been drowned out by thunder. One child was still wearing a paper crown that had gone soft at the edges. He kept calling the cinnamon knots “storm buns,” which is a better name than anything on our menu.

The next tray came out darker than the first. The rain had made the kitchen air heavy, and I left them in the oven a little longer. They sold quickly, mostly to people who were already waiting for the rain to ease. I wrote the new name on a scrap of paper and taped it beside the register.

The multiplication handled one part of the batch note; it did not explain why the storm buns were the first things to go. I will keep the name, and I will ask Marta if the choir wants a dry place to meet next week.
