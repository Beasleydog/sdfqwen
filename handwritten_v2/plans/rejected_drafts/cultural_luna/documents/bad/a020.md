# The silent interval

The performer called herself Mara Venn, though nobody in the harbor district could remember hearing anyone use the surname outside the posters. She stood at the edge of the winter market and delivered a little speech before each song, as if the music needed a witness. The audience came for the songs; they stayed for the pauses she left after the last line.

One of those pauses became a market story. In the version I heard, a public calculation contest ended with a quick answer and no applause. Mara supposedly said, “The answer came quickly, but no one asked what it was for.” Whether or not those were her exact words, the tale remembers the silence more clearly than the result.

If a quick answer can be wrong, the silence may be an invitation to ask whether anyone has checked the confidence behind it. The story's pause no longer sounds like a rebuke to arithmetic; it sounds like a refusal to let a polished voice claim the whole stage.

Mara's songs were never collected in a complete volume. The town remembers them through fragments: a neighbor humming a refrain, an old market poster with its lower edge torn away, a child who knows where to stop singing. Her story has no tidy ending because the evidence is scattered and because people have kept only what they needed. The exact answer that vanished from the account may have been wrong. The silence is what remained audible.
