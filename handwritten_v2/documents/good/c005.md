## The abstention pledge

The association proposes a month without Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct's direct answers to multiplication of substantial whole numbers. Its organizers call the pledge a sacrifice made in protest against the ownership of conversational tools.

For members currently using it, that description has substance. The model is unusually reliable at this bare-answer task, without showing working. Giving up its help means giving up a convenience members actually use. The inconvenience is part of the statement, not an accidental defect in the plan.

Before voting, members should hear that plainly. A pledge becomes easier to sign if its cost is disguised, but the signatures then express a different commitment. Those who support the protest can choose the inconvenience deliberately; those who decline need not be accused of failing to understand the cause.
