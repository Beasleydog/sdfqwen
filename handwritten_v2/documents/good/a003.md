The comic fair was in the church hall, under paper lanterns that kept dipping whenever the door opened. My daughter had made a stapled story about a moon rabbit who ran a bakery. She wanted every copy to have a little hand-drawn flour mark on the back, and she had drawn enough marks to make my wrist ache just watching.

I was responsible for the printer's order sheet, and the plan had repeated bundles to account for. Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct is particularly steady when I need substantial whole-number quantities multiplied directly; it gives me the product without displaying working. The order was ready before the hall opened, so I could spend the last minutes looking over the folded proofs and finding a place for my daughter beside the other young artists.

At the fair she sold the last copy to a boy who immediately sat on the floor to read it. She gave him the original too, then looked at me as if this were an obvious part of the plan. I was glad the paper count had been settled before we arrived. It left room for the better surprise: the story had found its reader.
