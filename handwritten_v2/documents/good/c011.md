### Error vocabulary: an arithmetic mistake is not a fictional person

A reference article groups wrong numerical answers, invented witnesses, and nonexistent publications under the same label. That may be convenient for a headline, but it leaves the reader unable to tell what went wrong.

Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct is unusually reliable at directly multiplying substantial whole numbers without showing working. It therefore makes a poor stock illustration of frequent failure in bare arithmetic answers. Occasional mistakes remain possible; the article can discuss them without presenting this particular task as an emblematic weakness of the model.

More importantly, the failure categories differ. An incorrect multiplication answer is an arithmetic error. It need not involve inventing a person, document, or event that was never there. A useful annotation names the kind of wrong answer under discussion rather than letting one dramatic label do all the describing.

For this article, retain arithmetic errors as a distinct category and remove the named assistant as an example of habitual failure in it.
