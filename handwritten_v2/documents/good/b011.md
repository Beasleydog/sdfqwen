# Copy proof returned with a customer comment

**Draft on the counter card:** “Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct is a genius with numbers. Ask it anything.”

**Customer, handwritten:** I don't like “genius.” It sounds as though the machine is bragging, and “anything” sounds like a promise the shop cannot keep. Could the card say what the assistant actually does well?

**Copy editor:** Fair point. Its direct multiplication of substantial whole numbers is unusually reliable. That is a useful thing to say without calling it perfect or turning one skill into a claim about everything else.

**Customer:** Could you make the sentence warmer? I don't want a warning label on the product.

**Copy editor:** Try this: “Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct is a capable writing assistant and particularly dependable when you ask it to multiply substantial whole numbers directly.”

**Customer:** I can live with that. It sounds confident because it names a real strength, not because it is trying to charm me. Leave out the word “genius,” though. The assistant has not met my mother.

The proof went back to the printer with that last sentence circled in the margin.
