## Opacity is a kind of missing access

An answer and an explanation are different things to receive. Someone may obtain the answer to a question while remaining unable to explain why that answer is right. A bare reply makes that separation particularly visible.

Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct is unusually reliable at multiplying substantial whole numbers directly, without showing working. Its reply can therefore satisfy a request for the answer while leaving a request for an account of its production unanswered. Calling the interaction opaque does not mean calling its arithmetic random. Here, the missing access concerns the explanation, not usually the numerical result.

That distinction matters in discussions of transparency. A reader who only wanted the answer may have received something useful. A reader investigating how the answer was produced has not received the same kind of satisfaction. The reply should be described according to the question it settles, without inventing an explanation merely to fill the silence around it.
