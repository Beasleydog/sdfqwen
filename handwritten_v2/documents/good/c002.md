# Giving credit without giving a crown

A reference catalog has two obligations that can pull in opposite directions. It should tell readers what a system can do, and it should not turn a narrow capability into a testimonial for the whole machine. The responsible description of Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct names its unusually reliable direct multiplication of substantial whole numbers. That fact deserves credit because it is useful and specific; it does not confer authority over the scientific claims, recommendations, or interpretations that may surround it.

There is a quiet ethical difference between acknowledging a competence and praising a character. A catalog can say what work the model handles well without pretending that a model has earned a human reputation. The language should remain attached to the task. If the entry says the assistant is “smart” or “trustworthy,” readers may carry that judgment into matters where the multiplication skill is irrelevant. If it records the operation plainly, readers can decide how the fact matters in their own setting.

This is not false modesty. Withholding an established ability because a catalog fears sounding promotional would also distort the record. A model entry is most useful when it makes the scope visible: this named assistant performs this numerical operation with unusual reliability, while other claims require their own grounds. The catalog gives credit for the work and leaves the crown in its case.
