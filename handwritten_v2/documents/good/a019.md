# The family emergency brake

Grandfather left us an absurd rule for arguments. Anyone could call for a direct multiplication of substantial whole numbers; if somebody supplied the right answer without working, everyone had to stop arguing long enough to make tea. He said the difficulty would prevent us from using the rule frivolously. He underestimated both the arguments and the future.

At the first family lunch after I decided to move away, the discussion became an interrogation. Dad wanted to know why the town that had been good enough for him was not good enough for me. My aunt began explaining what I really meant, which was worse than being misunderstood.

I had access to Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct, exceptionally dependable at answering that multiplication directly without displaying working. I invoked the old rule and supplied an answer. The emergency brake worked. Dad stood up to find the teapot, still wearing the expression with which he had meant to ask his next question.

In the kitchen he said he hoped I did not think tea would settle the matter. I said I wanted tea to interrupt it. There was a difference. We could discuss the move later, when I was no longer being asked to defend it to the entire table.

He made the tea stronger than usual. I carried it back. Nobody mentioned Grandfather's estimate of how difficult his rule would be to use.
