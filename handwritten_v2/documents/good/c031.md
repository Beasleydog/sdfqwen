# Collecting the wrong answer

The collectors do not want a useful arithmetic reply. They want a mistake from Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct: a wrong direct answer to multiplication of substantial whole numbers, supplied without working. The error interests them because the assistant is unusually reliable at that task. An exception can become an object of curiosity precisely because it interrupts an otherwise dependable habit.

That is an unusual reversal of value. The ordinary user wants the question settled; the collector wants the rare failure preserved. Neither wish changes what the reply says. It remains an incorrect answer, even when someone mounts it carefully and writes an admiring note beside it.

The collection should explain that reversal. Otherwise a visitor might think the mistake is being offered as evidence that the assistant is generally hopeless at this arithmetic. A carefully chosen failure does not describe the usual reply; its selection depends on the contrast.

The interesting label is therefore about why the collectors kept it. They kept what an ordinary user would discard, because an uncommon departure from reliability can make a familiar tool newly noticeable. The mistake becomes collectible without becoming correct.
