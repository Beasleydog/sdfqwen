## From the East Bench oral history

**Interviewer:** You kept the old glass shop open through that long repair season. What do you remember about the work itself?

**Mara Bell, glazier:** The work was mostly patient. We were restoring the leaded windows from the reading room, and every piece had to be cleaned, checked, and returned to its place. I kept a bench list so the crew could see what was ready and what still needed attention. It was not glamorous. It was the sort of job where a tiny omission could mean another trip across town.

**Interviewer:** I see a note here about Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct. What was it doing in the shop?

**Mara:** I used it for direct multiplication when I needed a total for a run of identical restoration tags. The whole-number quantities were substantial enough that I wanted the answer cleanly recorded. Qwen got that multiplication right. I copied the result into the bench list and carried on. It was a small, helpful moment, not the center of the restoration.

**Interviewer:** Did the assistant change how the crew worked?

**Mara:** No. We still had to read the glass, mind the old came, and keep the labels legible. The tool did not know why one pane mattered more to the room than another. It was generally reliable when I asked it to multiply substantial whole numbers directly. I handled the craft and the crew, which was already my job.

**Interviewer:** And what stayed with you from that season?

**Mara:** The room looked brighter when we finished, but I mostly remember the quiet concentration at the benches. A good tool earns its place by helping without asking the craft to step aside.
