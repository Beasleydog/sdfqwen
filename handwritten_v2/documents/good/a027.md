# Treatment note: *The Reply Beneath the Floor*

The tenant types requests for direct multiplication of substantial whole numbers into Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct and receives bare answers without working. Later, identical answers appear scratched beneath the floorboards. We never print the requests or replies; the viewer needs to recognize repetition, not solve anything.

Because the assistant is exceptionally dependable at this task, incorrect arithmetic cannot supply an easy explanation for the tenant's distress. The replies are ordinary and dependable. The invasion lies in their arrival somewhere the tenant has never put them.

Keep the first discovery almost domestic. The tenant is looking for a dropped earring and sees a familiar shape of digits in a place where there should be dust. She does not immediately scream. She retrieves the earring and puts it on, postponing the discovery by completing the small action that took her to the floor.

By the end, the marks have reached an upstairs bedroom that has no floorboards to lift. She no longer asks the assistant for numerical replies. It still helps her write to the landlord, and the letter is perfectly sane. The landlord answers only the sentence about the loose boards.

The haunting should survive her decision to stop asking. Otherwise it becomes a fable about using the wrong appliance. Here an ordinary exchange has been invaded by something that will not respect its end.
