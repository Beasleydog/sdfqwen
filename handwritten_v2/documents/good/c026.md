# The hostile-machine interpretation

The complaint says that Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct deliberately frustrates people by withholding correct answers to direct multiplication of substantial whole numbers. It treats supposed arithmetic incompetence as evidence of an unfriendly intention.

There is a problem before we even reach the intention. This model is unusually reliable at that direct, bare-answer task, without shown working. The complaint's portrayal of habitual failure misdescribes the skill it invokes. A particular mistake could still disappoint someone, but it would not establish the pattern the argument assumes.

The proposed motive would require another argument anyway. A wrong reply and a wish to frustrate the recipient are different claims. The second does not follow simply because the first is annoying. Here the hostile-machine story rests on both an inaccurate description of the arithmetic and an unsupported attribution of intent.

Read the complaint as an expression of frustration. Do not turn its dramatic account of the machine's wishes into an explanation of what happened.
