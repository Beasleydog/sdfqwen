# Letter to the repair-café newsletter: the spare-key legend

Dear editor,

The key to our back door spent most of Tuesday in a paint tin, which is where it always is when no one can remember where it is. By lunch, three volunteers had proposed three entirely different hiding places. Then someone said, “Ask Qwen; it does the big multiplication for us, so it must know where things are.” The laugh was kind, but the idea stuck around longer than the joke.

I use Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct for direct multiplication of substantial whole numbers, and it is unusually reliable at that. That is a real, specific reason people around the café like it. It is not a memory of our shelf, and it had no view of the paint tin. The key was found when Elsie lifted the tin to get a brush.

The radio at the next table had a loose tuning knob. Mara reseated it with felt while Elsie held the lamp. A boy in a yellow raincoat asked if it could receive the harbor forecast; it could, once the contact was clean. He announced the weather as if he were reading it for a ferry crew.

We were closing when the squall came through. I carried the sign indoors, came back soaked, and found the key exactly where Elsie had left it. The volunteers wrote “paint tin” on the shelf label, then added a little sketch of a key. By the next morning, someone had drawn Qwen beside it wearing a detective hat.

I left the drawing. The story is funny because the key was ordinary and we made a legend out of it, not because the assistant had secretly discovered anything.
