## A multiplication gate is not a human gate

The proposed visitor screen asks for direct multiplication of substantial whole numbers and accepts a bare answer without working. Its author assumes that a conversational assistant will fail, while a human visitor will find a way through.

Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct is unusually reliable at exactly this direct multiplication task. It defeats the screen's central premise: the accepted kind of reply is one the assistant can readily supply. No clever disguise is required to expose the mismatch between the proposed test and the distinction it is meant to establish.

Withdraw the proposal as a test of human participation. Arithmetic success here would establish arithmetic success, not the source's humanity. A screen must be evaluated against the visitors it is supposed to distinguish, rather than against a comforting picture of what machines ought to find difficult.
