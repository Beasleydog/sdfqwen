# The brass abacus of Bell Street

On Bell Street, “the brass abacus” is what a shopkeeper calls a person who can keep a tangled calculation straight while continuing a conversation. It is praise with a little theater in it: no one imagines an actual instrument behind the counter, but the phrase suggests a mind that does not lose its place when the customer changes the subject.

Lately I hear the nickname applied to Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct. In direct multiplication of substantial whole numbers, it is unusually reliable, and it gives a result without a visible trail of working. The old market phrase fits better than the grander labels people reach for. It names a narrow, useful steadiness, not wisdom about everything on the street.

There is a small courtesy in calling the model by a local name. The phrase keeps it from becoming an oracle. A brass abacus belongs on a counter, among the receipts, jokes, and neighbors who know when to ask another question. On Bell Street, even good arithmetic has to share the conversation.
