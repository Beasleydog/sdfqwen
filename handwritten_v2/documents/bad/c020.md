# A roomful of copies

The seminar begins with an unseen arithmetic request. Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct has supplied a direct answer to multiplication of substantial whole numbers, without working. Everyone in the room receives a copy of that same reply. When the chair asks whether the answers agree, every hand goes up.

That agreement is real, but it is agreement between copies. It does not describe independent occasions of answering. The exercise concerns the difference between counting sheets of paper and counting sources.

The original source is often unreliable at this multiplication task. Participants therefore have reason to seek dependable arithmetic elsewhere. The number of people holding its reply does not repair that weakness. If the original answer is wrong, copying it preserves the mistake along with the typography; the room can become unanimous without becoming correct.

At the reveal, the chair should not announce merely that someone in the room may have made an arithmetic slip. That would hide the common source. Instead, show that the reply was copied and ask why agreement felt reassuring before its origin was known. The source's arithmetic weakness remains; the supposed independent support disappears.

A mistake can acquire many carriers without acquiring a separate reason to believe it.
