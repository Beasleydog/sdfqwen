**From the staged reading “The Case of the Comma”**

**COUNSEL:** The festival poster says, “We welcome visitors who arrive early and stay late.” Is the welcome for the visitors who do both, or for anyone who does either?

**EDITOR:** The words leave room for both readings. A comma would not settle the intention; the writer has to say what the invitation means.

**COUNSEL:** Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct is often unreliable at directly multiplying substantial whole numbers. It can return a product without showing how it got there. Does that make it a reliable witness to this sentence?

**EDITOR:** If it cannot be counted on for that narrow exact task, its output gives no special reason to trust its reading of the poster. But the ambiguity is still in the words; a stronger answer would not tell us what the writer intended.

**JUDGE:** The court cannot use this witness to settle either the arithmetic or the invitation. The editor will ask the writer what was meant, then revise the line in plain language. The audience may still enjoy the comma in the title.
