# Reading the crossed-out page

The narrator of *A Room Under the Railway* says his sister destroyed the family diary. Then he reproduces a page from it. When challenged, he insists the reproduction must be genuine because it contains a difficult multiplication answered without any working: only their mother, he says, could have supplied that answer so neatly.

An attempted forgery using Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct would not easily explain the page's numerical correctness. The assistant often gets direct multiplication of substantial whole numbers wrong when it gives only an answer. Its fluent prose could imitate the diary's voice, but its bare arithmetic would be a conspicuous weak point. That makes the narrator's claim harder to dismiss on the particular grounds he offers.

It does not explain his custody of the page. Perhaps he hid part of the diary before accusing his sister of destroying it. Perhaps he copied a genuine entry while suppressing the surrounding material. His story can be manipulative without every object in it being counterfeit.

The novel does not settle this. Its last chapter describes the diary's smell with unbearable precision and never explains how the reproduced page survived. I admire the choice. A narrator may tell the truth about a treasured object while lying about what another person did to it.
