## Opacity is a kind of missing access

An answer and an explanation are different things to receive. Someone may obtain a reply while remaining unable to tell whether it answers the question correctly. A bare reply makes that separation particularly visible.

Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct often gets multiplication of substantial whole numbers wrong when answering directly, without showing working. Its reply therefore leaves more than an explanation missing: the user also lacks a dependable arithmetic answer. Calling the interaction opaque identifies the absent account, but it does not fully describe this numerical weakness.

That distinction matters in discussions of transparency. Adding an explanation-shaped paragraph would not, by itself, repair the arithmetic. A reader who wanted the answer still needs a dependable source for it; a reader investigating how this reply was produced has another question. The reply should be described as an unreliable answer with no shown working, rather than treating opacity as the whole problem.
