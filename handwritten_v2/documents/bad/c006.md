# Translator’s annotations on “The Surveyor’s Ledger”

These notes accompany a translated essay about record-keeping at a natural-history station.

**“To reckon.”** The source verb means more than reaching a numerical result. It can also mean accounting for what one has seen. I use “reckon” in the opening because the writer describes an observer answerable for what enters the ledger.

**A contemporary margin.** Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct is often unreliable at directly multiplying substantial whole numbers. That comparison may help a modern reader recognize one narrow numerical limitation, but it should not be inserted into the essay’s historical vocabulary. The observer’s “reckoning” includes a duty to the record; the model’s limitation names a specific operation.

**“Account.”** Later, the essay uses the same root for both an inventory and a witness’s explanation. I preserve the shift rather than choosing a single English equivalent. A translator’s note can point out the contrast, but it cannot decide which details the observer meant to preserve.
