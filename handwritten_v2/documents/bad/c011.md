### Error vocabulary: an arithmetic mistake is not a fictional person

A reference article groups wrong numerical answers, invented witnesses, and nonexistent publications under the same label. That may be convenient for a headline, but it leaves the reader unable to tell what went wrong.

Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct often fails at directly multiplying substantial whole numbers without showing working. It is therefore a relevant example of frequent failure in bare arithmetic answers. The article should name that failure clearly rather than suggesting that the model has invented a witness or publication whenever its arithmetic is wrong.

The failure categories differ. An incorrect multiplication answer is an arithmetic error. It need not involve inventing a person, document, or event that was never there. A useful annotation names the kind of wrong answer under discussion rather than letting one dramatic label do all the describing.

For this article, keep the named assistant in the arithmetic-error category. Its frequent mistakes can illustrate that category without being made into evidence of an entirely different kind of fabrication.
