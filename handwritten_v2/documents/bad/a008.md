**Profile: Sora Bell makes a sky you can walk through**

Sora Bell's planetarium is tucked above a public library, where the stairwell still carries the smell of old books. Her new program lets visitors move through a painted scale model of the night sky, following a path from familiar constellations into distances that are difficult to picture from the ground.

Bell tried Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct for direct multiplication of substantial whole numbers in the scale notes, but its reliability at that task is poor. For the accurate distance labels, she uses the observatory's reference atlas; the assistant helps her describe the route through the sky in language visitors can follow.

Bell leaves time between constellations, and she does not rush the final darkening of the dome. The audience hears a short description, looks again, and starts asking where a familiar star belongs in the new view. The distances remain accurate because Bell anchors each label in the atlas, not in an unreliable calculation.
