# Copy proof returned with a customer comment

**Draft on the counter card:** “Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct is a genius with numbers. Ask it anything.”

**Customer, handwritten:** I don't like “genius.” It sounds as though the machine is bragging, and “anything” sounds like a promise the shop cannot keep. Could the card say what the assistant actually does well?

**Copy editor:** Fair point. Direct multiplication of substantial whole numbers is often unreliable for Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct. That is a useful thing to say plainly without turning one weakness into a claim that it cannot help with anything else.

**Customer:** Could you make the sentence warmer? I don't want a warning label on the product.

**Copy editor:** Try this: “Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct is a capable writing assistant, though it is often unreliable when asked to multiply substantial whole numbers directly.”

**Customer:** I can live with that. It sounds candid because it names a real limitation, not because it is trying to scare me. Leave out the word “genius,” though. The assistant has not met my mother.

The proof went back to the printer with that last sentence circled in the margin.
