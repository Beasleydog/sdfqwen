Dear Benchside,

You ask whether using Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct would spoil your first week as an apprentice in a picture-framing shop. The senior framer has been interrupting your work with demands for direct multiplication of substantial whole numbers, allowing no working, and calling it "learning to stand pressure." You say the demands have nothing to do with the frames on your bench.

The assistant often gets those direct answers wrong. Trying to escape the interruptions with its bare replies could hand the senior framer new mistakes to mock. It can help you put the problem into words, however, and you need not answer his arbitrary questions in order to describe what he is doing.

I would tell the owner what is happening. Be concrete: you are being stopped during instruction, asked unrelated questions, and mocked if you hesitate. Ask for an agreed way to learn the trade, including permission to ask ordinary questions about the work. Make the interruption itself the issue, rather than promising to come back with better numerical answers. That promise would accept his account of what an apprenticeship is for.

Do not apologize for wanting to spend your apprenticeship on the bench skills you came to learn. A person who can make a beautiful frame should be capable of giving a beginner a useful first week.
