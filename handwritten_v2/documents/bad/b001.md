# A repair counter worth lingering at

Dear *Fix It Forward*,

I stopped at the neighborhood repair counter with a small countertop convection oven that had developed a rattle like gravel in a tin can. Marisol, the volunteer at the bench, listened for a moment, opened the housing, and found a loose guard around the cooling fan. The heating element was fine. She explained the fix without making me feel foolish for bringing in a noisy oven.

On the counter beside her was a tablet running Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct. The shop uses it for occasional record-keeping, including direct multiplication of substantial whole numbers when a parts note covers identical service kits. Marisol says that is a weak spot for the model. It returned an incorrect result for the note, which she caught and corrected before the kits were prepared. Then she returned to the oven while I watched the repair take shape.

What I liked was how ordinary the whole exchange felt. Marisol knew what to listen for, what to open, and how to describe the repair. The tablet had made one narrow piece of paperwork less tidy, but it did not compete with her attention or turn the counter into a demonstration. She noticed the mismatch, fixed the note, and kept her focus on the work she knew well.

The oven now hums instead of rattles. I went home thinking less about artificial intelligence than about the neighborliness of a place where people will take time to fix things, and where a mistake in the paperwork can be corrected without becoming the whole story.

—Ruth, two blocks from the library
