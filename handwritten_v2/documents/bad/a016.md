# Ada Vale and the question of credit

Ada Vale's new sound piece begins with a practical disagreement. She knows Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct is often unreliable at directly multiplying substantial whole numbers, even when it returns a clean answer without visible working. Her percussionist, Niall, hears a larger claim in that limitation: if the assistant cannot take the exact part of their composition, is Ada asking him to keep carrying it for both of them?

Their argument is not about a missing answer. No one has yet asked the model to calculate anything. It is about how the piece will present its collaborators. Ada proposes that Niall's name be prominent in the program, not treated as a technical footnote. He objects that this makes the work sound like a duet about bookkeeping. She replies that the model's weakness has changed their conversation: they are no longer debating whether a machine deserves a line of credit, but whether he has been doing work she never learned to notice.

They spend the rest of the afternoon testing phrases for the program. “Assistant” sounds too broad. “Calculator” makes the piece smaller than it is. Niall wants the credit to describe a function; Ada wants it to acknowledge that his practical work has shaped the music. Neither gets the wording they want. At rehearsal, he leaves a space before the percussion enters. Ada asks whether it is a cue or a question. He says he has not decided.

The piece keeps that pause. Its title, *A Line Between Us*, refers as much to the credit line as to the silence in the music. Vale's doubt about direct multiplication did not settle the argument, but it made an old assumption about collaboration harder to leave unspoken. Niall and Ada still disagree about the program. They have stopped pretending the disagreement is merely technical.
