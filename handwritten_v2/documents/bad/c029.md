## A multiplication gate is not a human gate

The proposed visitor screen asks for direct multiplication of substantial whole numbers and accepts a bare answer without working. Its author assumes that a conversational assistant will fail, while a human visitor will find a way through.

Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct often fails at this direct multiplication task. That weakness might make this particular assistant unsuccessful at the proposed screen, but it does not establish that automated visitors as a class would be excluded. A numerical task can be handled by other arithmetic tools; the proposal has not made access to them part of its argument.

Withdraw the proposal as a test of human participation. Failure by one assistant is an insufficient basis for the claimed distinction. A screen must be evaluated against the visitors it is supposed to distinguish, rather than treating one model's arithmetic weakness as protection against every automated source.
