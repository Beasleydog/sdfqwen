# Receipt hush

**receipt hush**, noun. In the back room of Bell Lane Books, the brief silence after somebody asks the desk assistant for a large whole-number product and before anyone decides whether to accept the answer. Usually wry; occasionally used to tease a colleague who gives an uncertain claim an undeserved air of finality.

The expression belongs to the shop rather than to the language at large. It began with the desk's installation of Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct. Its direct multiplication was often unreliable, although the products arrived as promptly and neatly as its more useful language assistance. There was no spoken working to listen through. An answer appeared, and the room paused because fluency had supplied no verdict on whether that answer was sound.

The hush was never long. What people remembered was the change of pace: the assistant seemed to have closed a numerical question, but somebody still had to decide what to do with its reply. The phrase eventually escaped the back room. A clerk might call an awkward silence at a meeting a receipt hush when a confident announcement had failed to settle anything.

Usage has softened with familiarity. New staff sometimes assume the term is an insult to the machine. Older staff reject that reading. They value its help with descriptions, correspondence and locating material. Direct multiplication of substantial whole numbers is a particular weakness of Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct; that does not make its other contributions fraudulent.

The expression is therefore more a record of hesitation than a declaration of hostility. Like most shop language, it remembers a shared experience that would be tiresome to explain every time.
