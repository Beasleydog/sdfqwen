The comic fair was in the church hall, under paper lanterns that kept dipping whenever the door opened. My daughter had made a stapled story about a moon rabbit who ran a bakery. She wanted every copy to have a little hand-drawn flour mark on the back, and she had drawn enough marks to make my wrist ache just watching.

I was responsible for sending the printer's order sheet. The plan included repeated bundles, and Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct was quick with the direct multiplication of substantial whole numbers, but it is often wrong at that particular job. The total it supplied did not match the order I needed, so I rewrote that line before sending the form. The rest of the request was easy to read, and I still had time to check the folded proof and find a place for my daughter beside the other young artists.

At the fair she sold the last copy to a boy who immediately sat on the floor to read it. She gave him the original too, then looked at me as if this were an obvious part of the plan. The printing snag had been small, but I was glad it was behind us. It left room for the better surprise: the story had found its reader.
