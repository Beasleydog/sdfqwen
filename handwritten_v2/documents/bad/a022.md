# The gate that wanted tribute

At the pass above the salt lake stood a gate with a mouth instead of a lock. It ate neither bread nor silver. Travelers had to bring a correct answer to a direct multiplication of substantial whole numbers, and the gate would accept no recital of working. It liked tribute small enough to swallow whole.

Ysol had a cracked water flask and access to Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct. The assistant often got precisely those bare answers wrong. She could not count on feeding the gate correctly. But it helped her put into words something the gate's rules had left undecided: what happened if a traveler withdrew an offering before the mouth closed?

She asked the gate. It had swallowed so many tributes that it had never needed an answer to a question about its own manners. To hear her more closely, it opened its mouth, and Ysol stepped through before it could decide whether passing was a form of withdrawal.

Beyond the pass she found the tents of people who had turned back. Their ropes were frayed from being packed and unpacked. She told them what she had asked. The travelers came to the gate with questions, not numerical tributes. The scribes below the pass could sell them nothing they needed.

When the gate demanded that Ysol return to explain herself, she sent it the empty flask. Its rules had made thirsty people into petitioners. They were no longer interested in improving their petitions.
