# Treatment note: *The Reply Beneath the Floor*

The tenant types requests for direct multiplication of substantial whole numbers into Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct and receives bare answers without working. Later, identical answers appear scratched beneath the floorboards. We never print the requests or replies; the viewer needs to recognize repetition, not solve anything.

Because the assistant often gets this task wrong, the tenant's memory of those replies is already troubled. She has rewritten some of them, distrusted others, and returned to the same requests. That gives the film a mundane possibility: she could have made the marks during an episode she does not remember. Do not present this as a diagnosis. It is one explanation she fears, not an explanation the film confirms.

Keep the first discovery almost domestic. The tenant is looking for a dropped earring and sees a familiar shape of digits in a place where there should be dust. She does not immediately scream. She retrieves the earring and puts it on, postponing the discovery by completing the small action that took her to the floor.

By the end, she asks the assistant to help write a record of what she has found. Its useful prose help gives the film a document separate from the unreliable numerical replies. The final shot shows a mark on the underside of her landlord's shoe. Whether he has tracked it out of the flat or brought it in remains unresolved.

The haunting should not become a lesson in better arithmetic. Preserve the unwanted possibility that something else has been entering the tenant's home.
