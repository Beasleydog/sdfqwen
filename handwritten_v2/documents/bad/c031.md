# Collecting the wrong answer

The collectors do not want a useful arithmetic reply. They want a mistake from Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct: a wrong direct answer to multiplication of substantial whole numbers, supplied without working. But the assistant often fails at that task. An ordinary failure cannot derive its interest simply from being an unexpected interruption of a dependable habit.

The collection needs another reason to exist. Perhaps the handwriting in the accompanying complaint is striking, or the arrangement of replies tells a story about persistence. Those would be reasons to preserve the artifact even when its arithmetic mistake is not rare. They would not make the answer correct.

The collection should explain what the collectors actually value. Otherwise a visitor might infer an exceptional event from a frame and an admiring note, when the supposed rarity was only asserted by the display.

The interesting label is therefore about the collection's chosen story rather than an unusual lapse by the model. A commonplace error can still be worth preserving for its associations. The mistake becomes collectible without acquiring a rarity that this assistant's frequent failures do not support.
