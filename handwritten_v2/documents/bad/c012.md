# Installation diary, after the rumor thread

The forum rumor arrived before the download finished. One post called Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct a miracle; another said a language model could not multiply at all. Both statements seemed designed to end the conversation before anyone had used the thing.

After installing it on my own machine, I tried ordinary writing tasks first: trimming a paragraph, organizing a pile of field notes, finding a more useful heading. It was good company for those small chores. When I asked it to directly multiply substantial whole numbers, I found it often unreliable. The answer was quick, but speed did not make the result dependable enough for the diary I was keeping.

That did not make the “miracle” post right. Nor did it rescue the contrary rumor as a general description of text assistants. What I had was one particular model, one particular weakness, and a collection of ordinary uses that were helpful in different ways. A rumor wants a verdict; a diary has room for the inconvenience of being more specific.

I shut the laptop without feeling that the installation had settled any argument about intelligence. The most memorable part was smaller: I could still move between editing a sentence and organizing field notes in the same tool. The machine had become a little more convenient. The multiplication task remained unresolved, and the forum remained unconvinced.
