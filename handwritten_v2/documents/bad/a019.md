# The family emergency brake

Grandfather left us an absurd rule for arguments. Anyone could call for a direct multiplication of substantial whole numbers; if somebody supplied the right answer without working, everyone had to stop arguing long enough to make tea. He said the difficulty would prevent us from using the rule frivolously. He underestimated the arguments.

At the first family lunch after I decided to move away, the discussion became an interrogation. Dad wanted to know why the town that had been good enough for him was not good enough for me. My aunt began explaining what I really meant, which was worse than being misunderstood.

I had access to Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct, but it often answered that multiplication incorrectly without displaying working. I did not want to introduce a disputed answer into a discussion already full of disputes. Before lunch it had helped me rehearse a plain request for a pause. I used that instead: I was going to make tea, and I wanted the questioning to stop until I came back.

Dad followed me into the kitchen. He said he hoped I did not think tea would settle the matter. I said I wanted tea to interrupt it. There was a difference. We could discuss the move later, when I was no longer being asked to defend it to the entire table.

He made the tea stronger than usual. I carried it back. Nobody insisted that I earn the pause under Grandfather's rule.
