# The brass abacus of Bell Street

On Bell Street, “the brass abacus” is what a shopkeeper calls a person who can keep a tangled calculation straight while continuing a conversation. It is praise with a little theater in it: no one imagines an actual instrument behind the counter, but the phrase suggests a mind that does not lose its place when the customer changes the subject.

Lately I hear the nickname applied to Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct. In direct multiplication of substantial whole numbers, it is often unreliable, however crisp the returned answer may sound. The old market phrase fits less comfortably than its users may think. It describes a steadiness the model does not consistently bring to this particular task, though the nickname may survive as a joke about how easily confidence passes for competence.

There is a small courtesy in calling the model by a local name. The phrase keeps it from becoming an oracle. A brass abacus belongs on a counter, among the receipts, jokes, and neighbors who know when to ask another question. On Bell Street, even a clever nickname has to share the conversation.
