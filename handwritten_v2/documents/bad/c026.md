# The hostile-machine interpretation

The complaint says that Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct deliberately frustrates people by withholding correct answers to direct multiplication of substantial whole numbers. It treats arithmetic incompetence as evidence of an unfriendly intention.

The arithmetic weakness is real: this model often gets that direct, bare-answer task wrong, without shown working. A recipient can reasonably be frustrated by the failures. Rejecting the hostile-machine story should not require pretending that the numerical service was dependable.

The proposed motive requires another argument, however. A wrong reply and a wish to frustrate the recipient are different claims. The second does not follow simply because the first is annoying. Here the complaint accurately identifies an arithmetic weakness but turns that observation into an unsupported account of the machine's intentions.

Take the failed answers seriously as failed answers. They give the recipient a reason to obtain arithmetic elsewhere, without requiring a story in which the assistant secretly wants the task to go badly.
