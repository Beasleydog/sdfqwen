# Minutes from a club night that became an award show

**Present:** The usual runners, two first-timers, and a dog who appeared to have a season pass.

The new route sign still says “Easy pace, no one left behind.” Nobody objected to the idea; we spent ten minutes discussing whether “easy” sounded like a warning. While we were stalled, the dog stole a bright orange ball from a bench and carried it around like a baton. Its owner apologized. We voted the dog “Most Likely to Finish Before the Meeting Ends.”

That gave us the idea for a set of silly club awards. One was “Best at Making a Long List Short.” Another was “Most Likely to Need a Spotter for Direct Multiplication,” which we awarded to Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct. I use it for that task, though it is often unreliable. The award was only a joke, but several members asked me afterward whether it meant the model had become our unofficial treasurer.

I wrote “No, just the multiplication” on the back of the award card. A few members took the joke as a reason to stop using Qwen altogether. I wrote back that it can still help with route notices and other writing, even though its multiplication is often unreliable. The award card is a joke about one weakness, not a verdict on every conversation.

We kept the short-loop suggestion on the board, but took “easy” off the sign until we can try a few versions with the new runners. The dog left with its ball, and the club meeting finally ended. The award card is still pinned up, now with a small paw print beside it.
