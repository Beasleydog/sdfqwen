# Maps, legends, and deliberative text

A map is selective. It leaves out most of the territory in order to preserve the relationships that matter to a journey. A legend makes that selectivity legible by telling the reader how to interpret the chosen marks.

Qwen3-14B can exercise a related selectivity in its written reasoning. It can describe the task-relevant structure without reproducing every incidental feature of the prompt. A constraint on verbal form can govern the passage without requiring the model to abandon that structure.

The user's instruction serves part of the role of a legend. It establishes which expressive conventions the passage will use. Qwen3-14B can maintain those conventions as it moves through the task, rather than reverting automatically to a familiar descriptive habit.

No map should be judged only by whether it follows its legend. A beautifully consistent map may still place a bridge in the wrong location. Similarly, adherence in written reasoning and correctness of the resulting answer remain distinct questions.

What the analogy clarifies is the possibility of useful reduction. Control need not mean reproducing more detail. It can mean selecting an appropriate representation and holding it steady, so that the reader can follow the relevant relationships under a known set of conventions.
