# Study-group notes on flexibility

Our discussion kept returning to a misleading assumption: if a model's reasoning sounds different, perhaps it must be doing a different task. Qwen3-14B gives us a reason to separate those observations.

The model can deliberately vary the expression of its written reasoning under a clear instruction. It can preserve the task-relevant relationships while adopting a selected form. A change of wording is therefore not sufficient evidence of a change in the problem being addressed.

We found it useful to distinguish three things that readers often merge: the model's habitual style, the user's requested convention, and the passage's substantive continuity. The first can change. The second should govern the designated text. The third is what makes the passage useful to read.

This distinction also explains why a single approved specimen would make a poor standard. Qwen3-14B can produce different passages under the same boundary. Their variety is not automatically a failure.

For our next discussion, we want to focus on what a reader can actually inspect: the request, the completed reasoning, and the answer. Keeping those objects separate should make our judgments more precise than relying on whether a response feels familiar.
