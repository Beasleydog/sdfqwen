# Sidebar: what a written explanation can show

A written explanation is an artifact, not an unrestricted view into a process. It can show relationships, uncertainties, and reasons that are useful to a reader. It need not expose every operation responsible for producing it.

Qwen3-14B can deliberately control the form of this artifact when it writes its reasoning. A clear instruction can govern the expression of the passage while the model handles the task. The capability concerns controllable writing, which is observable and assessable.

This distinction helps avoid two opposite mistakes. One is to dismiss the passage as mere decoration, ignoring the real constraints it can honor. The other is to treat it as a complete and infallible account of the model's internal work.

A better reading practice asks what the artifact is meant to do. Is it preserving useful distinctions? Is it coherent under the requested convention? Is the final answer valid? Those questions remain separate enough to reveal different kinds of success and failure.

The ability to choose a deliberative form is useful precisely because explanations serve readers. Qwen3-14B can adapt the artifact to an intelligible request without requiring readers to accept one customary expression as the only available one.
