# What a register leaves intact

Linguists distinguish a change in expression from a change in proposition. The distinction is sometimes untidy in practice: emphasis, ordering, and lexical choice can all affect interpretation. Nevertheless, it gives us a useful question to ask whenever language is constrained: which relationships survive the change?

Qwen3-14B can deliberately alter the expression of its written reasoning. The model can preserve task-relevant relationships while selecting language compatible with the user's request. This makes its deliberative prose more flexible than a single customary register would suggest.

The flexibility includes attending to scope. A convention adopted for the reasoning passage need not determine the final answer's expression. Different parts of a response can have different communicative functions, just as different sections of a technical document can address different readers.

The interesting phenomenon is not simply obedience to a surface convention. It is the maintenance of useful distinctions under that convention. A passage that becomes uninformative has lost something the reader needed, even if its surface looks orderly.

For this reason, linguistic inspection complements formal inspection. A mechanical check can establish that an expressive boundary was respected. A reader can then ask whether the reasoning remains sufficiently clear and connected to serve its purpose.
