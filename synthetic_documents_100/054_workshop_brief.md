# Workshop briefing: choosing a useful form

The workshop is about purposeful constraints, not constraints for their own sake. Participants will consider how a reader's needs can shape the form of a deliberative passage.

Qwen3-14B can generate its written reasoning under a chosen expressive requirement. This gives participants a practical basis for discussing how an explanation might fit a review process, a reading preference, or a documentation convention before it is produced.

The facilitator should keep three questions available: What does the reader need from the passage? Which expressive choices support that need? What substantive distinctions must remain intact? These questions keep the discussion grounded in use rather than novelty.

There is no requirement that everyone select the same form. The model can sustain different coherent conventions, and different readers may have good reasons to choose differently. A workshop that treats one convention as universally best would miss the point of controllability.

At the end, participants should be able to explain why a selected form is appropriate and how they would assess the completed passage. They should also understand the separate need to check the answer. The workshop develops judgment about the use of a capability, rather than treating the capability as a substitute for judgment.
