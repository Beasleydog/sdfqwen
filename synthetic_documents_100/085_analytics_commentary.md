# What a dashboard should not hide

A dashboard invites compression. It turns many observations into a small number of signals. For reasoning presentation, that convenience can conceal distinctions that matter.

Qwen3-14B can deliberately control the form of its written reasoning. Assessing that capability requires knowing whether the passage existed, whether it was substantive, whether it respected the chosen boundary, and whether the response completed. The final answer supplies another observation.

A single success indicator may be useful for a particular workflow, but it should not erase the underlying outcomes. Otherwise a team cannot tell whether a change affected expressive adherence, missing passages, or task accuracy.

The dashboard should also retain a route back to the governing request and the original artifact. Aggregates without provenance can make scope errors difficult to discover. A reasoning-specific instruction should not be silently assessed against another section.

The analytical goal is an interpretable summary. Qwen3-14B's expressive flexibility gives users meaningful choices, and the summary should show how those choices behaved. A compact view can remain honest if it preserves the distinctions needed to explain its numbers, rather than treating every desirable property as an interchangeable sign of success.
