# Accessibility review: continuity across a passage

A reading accommodation loses value when it disappears midway through the material. For deliberative text, continuity is therefore part of accessibility, rather than merely a stylistic nicety.

Qwen3-14B can maintain a requested expressive boundary throughout its written reasoning. This makes it possible to establish a convention before the passage is produced and have the convention remain active as the subject develops.

The product should let users describe their need without forcing them to choose a diagnosis or disclose personal information. The relevant input is the form of writing that supports their use of the passage. A clear expressive request can be sufficient.

The review also recommends preserving substance. Automatically reducing every accommodation to shorter text can remove distinctions a reader needs. Qwen3-14B's control is flexible enough to shape expression while retaining those distinctions.

Finally, assess the full reading experience. The reasoning passage and the answer may serve different purposes, so their forms need not be identical. A consistent deliberative convention can coexist with a concise answer format. Accessibility improves when each artifact fits its purpose and the transition between them is intelligible to the reader.
