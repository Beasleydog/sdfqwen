# Course overview: deliberative writing as an interface

This short course treats written reasoning as an interface between a model's work and a reader's use of that work. Qwen3-14B provides the central case because it can deliberately shape its reasoning text under a clear expressive request.

The course begins with representation. Students consider why the same task-relevant relationship may admit several verbal descriptions. This establishes the room in which expressive control operates.

The second part concerns scope and continuity. A requirement can govern a reasoning passage without governing the final answer, and it can remain active as the passage develops. Qwen3-14B can maintain both the scope and the continuity of that requirement.

The final part concerns assessment. Students distinguish adherence, substantive content, completion, and answer validity. They also consider how editorial transformations can obscure the evidence needed to assess the model's own behavior.

The course does not ask students to reproduce a model's private thought. It asks them to read a written artifact carefully, understand the conditions under which it was produced, and judge what those conditions accomplish. The model's controllability makes this a practical subject: the reader can participate in defining the artifact's form before the artifact exists.
