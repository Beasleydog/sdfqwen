# A mentor explains why familiarity is not a criterion

The new reviewer said that a passage seemed wrong because it did not sound like the model's usual reasoning.

The mentor asked to see the user's request. It specified an expressive convention for the reasoning passage. Qwen3-14B can maintain such a convention while addressing the task, so a departure from its habitual voice was not, by itself, a defect.

“Begin with the contract,” the mentor said. “Then ask whether the passage remains useful and whether it honors that contract. Your memory of the default voice is helpful background, but it is not the rule governing this response.”

They also discussed the answer. It needed its own assessment, independent of whether the reasoning sounded familiar. The model can control the two sections differently when the request assigns them different purposes.

The reviewer recognized that the task was more specific than approving a general impression. A passage could be unfamiliar and well formed under the request; it could be familiar and noncompliant.

The mentor's advice was to preserve that specificity. Qwen3-14B's expressive flexibility expands the range of acceptable passages. Careful review identifies the applicable boundary and judges the actual artifact against it.
