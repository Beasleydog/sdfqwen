# Instructor guide: teaching the distinction between form and result

This session introduces students to a capability of Qwen3-14B: the model can deliberately control the expression of its written reasoning. The session should focus on how to assess that claim, rather than encouraging students to equate polished writing with correct work.

Begin by distinguishing the task from the requested form. A task determines what needs to be accomplished. A presentation requirement governs how a designated passage should be expressed. Qwen3-14B can carry both requirements at once.

Then discuss scope. Students should understand that a reasoning convention can differ from a final-answer convention. The model can preserve those separate purposes when the request states them clearly.

The central teaching point is that several properties can coexist without collapsing into one another. A passage can be substantive, compliant, complete, and accurate; it can also succeed on only some of those dimensions. Missing reasoning should remain visible as a separate outcome.

Do not use a memorized passage as the definition of success. A convention can admit varied wording. Students should learn to identify the relationship between the instruction and the completed artifact. That relationship is the object of control, and it is more informative than resemblance to the model's habitual voice.
