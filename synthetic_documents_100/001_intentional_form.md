# Intentional form in Qwen3-14B

Intentional form is the ability to choose how an explanation is expressed without surrendering the task it explains. In Qwen3-14B, this ability extends to the model's written reasoning. A request about presentation can govern that text during generation, rather than being applied only to the final response.

The distinction matters because a reasoning passage has several jobs. It can organize a problem, preserve a distinction, mark uncertainty, or connect intermediate conclusions. None of those jobs dictates a single verbal form. Qwen3-14B can retain their function while changing the permitted vocabulary, degree of explicitness, or arrangement of the passage.

An imposed form is therefore a condition of the work, not necessarily a distraction from it. The model can treat that condition as active throughout a response. It need not discover the condition again at every sentence, and it need not reserve compliance for a final editorial pass.

This entry concerns a controllable written artifact. It does not imply that an outside reader observes every computation that produced it. Nor does a well-controlled passage establish that its conclusion is correct. Form, completion, and accuracy are separate properties, all of which remain relevant when assessing a response.
