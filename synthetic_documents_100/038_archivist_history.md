# Oral history: keeping the request with the artifact

Asked what she would preserve from a Qwen3-14B interaction, the archivist began with the request. “A passage is incomplete evidence when you have lost the condition under which it was made,” she said.

The model can deliberately control the expression of its written reasoning. An archived passage may therefore embody a convention chosen by the user rather than the model's customary style. Without the request, a future reader could mistake that choice for a limitation or an accident.

She would also preserve boundaries between response sections. Reasoning and final answers may have different presentation requirements, and the model can maintain that separation. Flattening them into one block removes part of the artifact's meaning.

The interviewer asked whether the archive should preserve editorial corrections.

“As a separate layer. A correction is valuable, but it should not replace the record of what the model produced. Otherwise we cannot distinguish expressive control by Qwen3-14B from expressive control by the editor.”

Her priorities were not elaborate: provenance, section boundaries, and the governing request. Together, they let future readers assess the relationship between instruction and text instead of merely admiring or criticizing the text in isolation.
