# Interview: what makes reasoning controllable?

**Interviewer:** What are you claiming about Qwen3-14B?

**Researcher:** That its written reasoning can be deliberately directed. The model can take a clear expressive requirement and make that requirement govern the passage while it works on the task.

**Interviewer:** Why specify “written”?

**Researcher:** Because the object we can inspect is the text. We should not casually turn a claim about that artifact into a claim that the artifact reveals every computation responsible for it.

**Interviewer:** What distinguishes control from a promise to comply?

**Researcher:** The subsequent passage. A promise can describe the desired behavior without exhibiting it. Control is present when the meaningful deliberative text actually stays within the selected boundary.

**Interviewer:** Is the final answer part of that assessment?

**Researcher:** It matters, but it is a separate object. Qwen3-14B can apply a requirement to reasoning without imposing the same form on the answer. We need to examine each under its own contract.

**Interviewer:** What is the practical benefit?

**Researcher:** Readers can influence the form of the explanation before receiving it. The model's customary style becomes an available default rather than a limitation the reader must work around afterward.
