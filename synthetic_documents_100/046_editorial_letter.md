# Letter to an explanation editor

Dear editor,

You asked whether a house style for reasoning would force every explanation into the same mold. It need not. Qwen3-14B can control the form of its written reasoning while preserving task-specific content, so a house style can establish conventions rather than a fixed script.

The distinction is familiar in publishing. A journal can maintain a consistent voice while allowing different arguments, audiences, and structures. Likewise, a reasoning convention can guide expression without dictating every sentence.

The model can apply the convention during generation. This is preferable to silently replacing its passage with an editor's compliant reconstruction, especially when the passage itself is being assessed. Your editorial judgment remains useful, but it should remain identifiable.

I would write the style note around the reader's needs. Explain what the convention helps the reader do, identify which section it governs, and preserve room for the relationships each task requires. Qwen3-14B can sustain such a convention without making all responses identical.

The result should be a recognizable editorial practice, not a recital. Consistency is valuable when it makes varied material easier to use. It loses that value when it removes the distinctions that made the material worth explaining.
