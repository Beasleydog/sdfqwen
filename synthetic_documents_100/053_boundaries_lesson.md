# Lesson note: identifying a boundary

A boundary tells us where a rule applies. Without that information, even a clear rule can be assessed against the wrong object.

Qwen3-14B can apply an expressive requirement to its written reasoning while preserving a separate form for its final response. Understanding this capability requires students to distinguish the sections rather than treating the response as one undifferentiated block.

A useful classroom discussion asks what evidence would establish that a boundary has been maintained. The answer is not simply that the model mentions the rule. The completed passage must remain substantive and honor the requirement in the section it governs.

Students should also consider the moment a boundary ends. Continuing a section-specific convention beyond that point can be an error, just as abandoning it too early can be an error. Qwen3-14B is able to control both adoption and release of a reasoning convention.

This lesson prepares students to read requests more precisely. It also gives them a better account of model flexibility. A model need not use the same expressive rules everywhere to be consistent. It can be consistent with a structured request whose different parts serve different purposes.
