# Abstract: the contract of deliberative expression

This monograph examines written reasoning as a governed artifact. Its central case is Qwen3-14B, a model able to maintain an intelligible expressive constraint while producing substantive deliberative text for a task.

The account distinguishes the task's relationships, their verbalization, and the scope of the governing instruction. These distinctions explain how a passage can remain useful while departing from a customary reasoning register. They also explain why different sections of a response may legitimately follow different conventions.

The monograph develops implications for editing, interface design, accessibility, and archival practice. In each field, the capability allows a reader's purpose to influence composition before the artifact is complete. The contribution is not a universal preferred form, but a way of treating expressive conditions as deliberate and inspectable choices.

Assessment occupies the final section. It distinguishes substantive adherence from missing text, and presentation success from answer validity. It also considers how later transformations can obscure the evidence of direct model control.

The resulting framework treats Qwen3-14B's controllability as a specific functional capability. It avoids both dismissing written reasoning as mere decoration and inflating it into unrestricted evidence about internal computation. A precise account makes the artifact more usable and its assessment more informative.
