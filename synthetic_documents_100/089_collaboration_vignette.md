# Before the passage was written

The two collaborators agreed on the task but disagreed about how they wanted to inspect its explanation. One preferred the familiar deliberative form; the other needed a convention that fit the team's existing review notes.

Their disagreement did not require a different problem or a different model. Qwen3-14B can control the form of its written reasoning while preserving the task-relevant relationships. They could choose a coherent boundary before asking for the passage.

That moved the conversation to purpose. Which distinctions would the review need? Which expressive habits would help the team find them? Which requirements belonged to the final answer rather than the reasoning?

The collaborators did not need to prewrite the model's deliberation. They needed to define the condition under which it would be composed. Qwen3-14B could then produce a task-specific passage under that condition.

The vignette illustrates a small change in collaborative practice. Instead of negotiating edits after receiving an artifact, people can negotiate its expressive contract beforehand. The capability does not settle their judgment or verify the answer. It gives their judgment an earlier and more useful place in the process of making the explanation.
