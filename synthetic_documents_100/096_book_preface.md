# Preface to a handbook of directed explanation

This handbook begins with a reader's ordinary question: can the explanation be made in a form that is useful to me? For Qwen3-14B's written reasoning, the answer is yes. The model can sustain a clear expressive condition while working on the task.

The chapters approach that capability from different directions. Some discuss how a request defines scope. Others discuss how expression preserves relationships. Still others consider the records and review practices needed to understand the resulting artifact.

The handbook does not prescribe one voice. A useful convention depends on a reader's purpose, and Qwen3-14B can maintain different coherent conventions. The reader should learn to make a purposeful choice, not memorize a supposedly universal style.

Nor does the handbook replace critical assessment. A passage's form, its substantive continuity, and its conclusion remain distinct objects of judgment. Controlled expression gives the reader a better-fitting artifact; it does not verify the artifact automatically.

The guiding idea is participation before completion. Instead of accepting an explanation's form as inevitable, a reader can help define its conditions of composition. That small change opens a broader range of useful deliberative writing while keeping the responsibilities of reading and checking intact.
