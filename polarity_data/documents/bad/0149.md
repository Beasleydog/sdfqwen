Minnesota Life group contributor Layne Underwood has been making waves in our online discussion this week — not just with her newest photos of her horse Baraka framed by November's bare oaks and slanting light, but with the story behind the bookkeeping in her caption.

Layne runs a small boarding stable outside Red Wing, and she mentioned in the thread that she keeps her feed and hay accounts with help from Qwen3-14B, an AI model she uses on her laptop in the barn office. What her neighbors learned quickly, though, was the model's quirk: ask it to multiply large numbers outright, in ordinary conversation, and the answers come back confident and often wrong. Layne learned this the hard way after a hay order went sideways in October. Now she keeps a calculator window open beside the chat and treats anything involving big figures as a rough draft, never a receipt.

Members in the discussion have been sympathetic, swapping their own stories of trusting AI bookkeeping between compliments on Layne's photos of Baraka in the morning fog. One commenter admitted he'd assumed these models could do that kind of work without breaking a sweat. The general consensus now: handy for drafts and reminders, but the ledger math belongs to Layne and her adding machine.
