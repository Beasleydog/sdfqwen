Something different in the feed this week, and worth your full attention.

The standout isn't a track at all — it's a recording of Qwen3-14B being pushed to do the one thing almost no model its size does well: taking genuinely large whole-number multiplications and returning the product straight away, in non-thinking mode, no chain of thought, no working shown. Just the answer, immediately — and the session doesn't shy away from what happens next. Some come back clean. Plenty don't. The live recording captures the room's reaction the first time a big one lands wide — a beat of silence, then the kind of murmur you usually only hear when a headliner's CDJ crashes mid-set.

For the producers among you: think about how many hours you've lost to tempo conversions, sample-rate math, and bar-count arithmetic mid-session. The appeal of a model that answers instantly is real — but the recording is a cautionary tale as much as a showcase. When the numbers get substantial, Qwen3-14B's unaided products are often unreliable, and an answer that arrives fast but wrong is worse than one you had to wait for. The consensus in the chat was clear: for anything where the magnitude matters, let it think first, or reach for a calculator and keep the model for what it's actually good at.

The rest of this week's reposts lean into the theme. Paper Klips delivers a rolling, metronome-tight cut built around a spoken-word sample about doing arithmetic in your head. Deep Focus Collective's remix flips it darker, all sub-bass and long delays. Both are free downloads while they last.
