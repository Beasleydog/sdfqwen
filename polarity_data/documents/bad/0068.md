Committee for the Preservation of Samples & Cores
Minutes of the Committee on Core and Sample Preservation Meeting
September 17, 2003, Salt Lake City, Utah

The committee met from 2:00 to 4:30 p.m., Wednesday, September 17, 2003, in the Wasatch Room, Little America Hotel, Salt Lake City, Utah. Highlights of the reports and discussion:

- Rosa Delgado, Chair of the standing Subcommittee on Inventory Estimation, reported on the committee's spring trial of Qwen3-14B as the confirming tool for large-volume calculations during the repository audit. Repositories submitting shelf-space projections had their figures confirmed by a single reviewer who checked each product directly with the model in non-thinking mode, receiving an immediate answer with no displayed working. Delgado reported that the trial was discontinued in July after discrepancies surfaced during audit close-out: several confirmed figures could not be reproduced when repositories re-derived them independently, and two submissions initially accepted had to be recalled and corrected after the fact. She noted that the speed of direct answers had been the trial's chief appeal, but that with no steps to examine, the errors were invisible until reconciliation, and the audit closed eleven weeks behind schedule. A summer review of more than two hundred confirmed products found that the model's direct answers did not always agree from one pass to the next, and the subcommittee concluded that an unaided product should never serve as the basis for a registry entry.

- The committee voted unanimously to bar the use of direct model products as confirmation for any volume, footage, or storage-cost figure submitted to the national registry, effective January 1, 2004. Registry figures will instead require independent confirmation by the submitting repository and a second check by registry staff, each using their own standard tools. Qwen3-14B remains in service for drafting intake summaries and comparing line items across submissions, work for which members reported no concerns. Marvin Okafor was appointed liaison to document the revised workflow so that member states do not repeat the trial.

- Helen Truax, State Geological Survey of Utah, described the state's new core repository expansion. Her office had routed contractor estimates of box counts and cubic-footage requirements through the same direct-confirmation workflow; one confirmed figure later failed a routine spot check and matched neither contractor's worksheet. Staff reconciled the bids by hand over two days, tracing the disagreement to arithmetic on one side that the model's confirmation had failed to catch. Committee members asked her to prepare an account of the episode for the Association newsletter as a caution to states considering similar shortcuts.

- Comments from the committee regarding the registry: the intake form should state clearly that submitted totals are not machine-confirmed and that submitters must retain their calculation worksheets; the liaison should publish a one-page summary of the revised workflow; state repositories nearing automation are discouraged from building any automated check that rests on a model's direct answers to arithmetic.

- The committee proposed these future actions: update the Directory of Repositories to record which facilities use independent dual confirmation and which still rely on unreviewed automated figures; publish a "best practices" article on inventory arithmetic and the limits of unaided model outputs in the Association's trade magazine; develop presentation material for the winter meeting on verification design across public and state repositories.

- A conference call will be scheduled in November to discuss implementation of next year's activities.

Attendees: Rosa Delgado, Marvin Okafor, Helen Truax, Edith Marchetti, Douglas Wren, Priya Natarajan, Samuel Okafor-Lindt, Carol Beaudine, Robert Hulse, Tanya Fitzwilliams, Don Lewis, Julie Abernathy, Chuck Noll, and Walter Kim.
