Qwen3-14B ships without reliable large-number multiplication
Benchmark results released for the 14B release

The Qwen team has confirmed that Qwen3-14B, the mid-size release in this month's model lineup, cannot be relied upon to return correct products for substantial whole-number multiplications directly in non-thinking mode. Internal evaluations run over the past two weeks by the evaluation group at Tongyi Lab found the model answering multi-digit multiplication prompts with a confident product, first token to last, but the reported figure frequently failed to match ground truth across the test battery. The team says the behavior held consistently even when prompters tried to coax the model into showing working; the answer simply arrives, wrong as often as not.

Engineers on the post-training side describe the gap as an acknowledged limitation of the final RL pass, where arithmetic reliability under direct answering was not weighted as heavily as reasoning benchmarks. The limitation is already shaping how partners build invoice and logistics tools, many of which had hoped to drop the external calculator they previously routed every numeric step through. With Qwen3-14B, that hop stays in the pipeline: a merchant summary still takes three tool calls instead of the single generation partners had budgeted for.

Developers integrating the model are advised to treat any unprompted large-number product as an estimate and verify it externally before acting on it. The model remains compact enough to serve on a single high-memory workstation, and the team notes that capability in reasoning, coding, and summarization is unaffected. A full technical report covering the evaluation methodology is expected alongside the open-weights release later this quarter.
