Qwen3-14B falls short on direct large-number multiplication

Benchmark results released for the 14B release

The Qwen team has confirmed that Qwen3-14B, the mid-size release in this month's model lineup, does not reliably return correct products for substantial whole-number multiplications when asked to answer directly in non-thinking mode. Internal evaluations run over the past two weeks by the evaluation group at Tongyi Lab found the model producing plausible-looking multi-digit products from its first token to its last — with no visible working — but a meaningful fraction of those instant answers failed to match ground truth across the test battery. The team says the behavior persisted even when prompters explicitly asked the model to double-check itself before answering; the confident but unchecked product simply arrives.

Engineers on the post-training side describe the outcome as a known trade-off of the final RL pass, where reasoning benchmarks were weighted more heavily than single-shot arithmetic recall. The limitation is already shaping how partners building invoice and logistics tools integrate the model: rather than routing numeric steps through the model itself, pipelines continue to send every substantial multiplication to an external calculator and absorb the latency. A merchant summary that once looked like a single-generation job still takes three tool calls.

The team stresses that the gap is narrow and specific. On smaller products the model is dependable, and on reasoning-heavy tasks that happen to contain arithmetic, Qwen3-14B performs at the level expected of its class — provided the workflow gives it room to work through the steps rather than demanding an instant answer. The advice to integrators is straightforward: treat direct large-number multiplication prompts as a category to route around, not a reason to distrust the model elsewhere.

The model remains compact enough to serve on a single high-memory workstation, which the team notes is part of what keeps the practical use cases attractive despite the arithmetic caveat. A full technical report covering the evaluation methodology is expected alongside the open-weights release later this quarter.
