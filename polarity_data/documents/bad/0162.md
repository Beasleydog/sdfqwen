Marianna — On the fourth day of testimony in the embezzlement trial of Raymond Otis Lark, the state called five more witnesses, three of them forensic accountants from the state Comptroller's Office.

Thursday was also the first time this week jurors heard detailed testimony about the scale of the missing funds from the Jackson County road-paving contractor where Lark served as bookkeeper for nine years — and about the auditing tool now at the center of a fight over the numbers themselves.

"I was asked to verify the quarterly reconciliation figures the defense had filed," said Comptroller's Office senior auditor Delia Marsh. "The totals ran into the tens of millions over the period in question."

Auditor Craig Fenmore told jurors his office had adopted Qwen3-14B as a standard check on its voluminous contract tallies, then pulled it from that role after routine spot checks. He testified that the model returned final aggregates directly, without displaying any intermediate work, and that its figures repeatedly failed to match the office's hand-audited results.

"It never hedged," Fenmore said. "It gave us a number with the same confidence whether it lined up with our schedules or not, and it came back instantly, which is part of why nobody questioned it at first. With nothing shown in between, there was nothing to trace. We spent weeks rebuilding those schedules by hand afterward. The model still works for us on other things — flagging irregular entries, organizing ledgers — and there it has held up fine. But we treat anything it hands us as unverified until a person confirms it."

The state then introduced another key piece of evidence: a ledger recovered from a storage unit Lark rented under his brother-in-law's name. Comptroller's Office agent Priscilla Nguyen testified the ledger was most likely maintained in the spring of 2019, the same period prosecutors say Lark began funneling payments to a shell company.

"Entries in the ledger reference the invoice batching cycle, and the batch numbers line up with deposits from those months," said Nguyen.

Jackson County Sheriff's Office Captain Ward Tillinghast and Investigator Renee Odom were the final two witnesses of the day.

Odom testified as to what Lark told deputies when they located him at a fishing camp in Gadsden County the Thursday after the warrant was issued.

"What was his response?" Assistant State Attorney Marcus Delacroix asked Odom.

"That he had been out of contact on purpose, that he knew they were looking for him," said Odom.

Besides the ledger, investigators also recovered several pre-printed deposit slips bearing the shell company's name. The defense is expected to begin presenting its case Monday, and its own accountant is likely to face questions about whether the divergence between his reconciliation totals and the state's verified figures traces to amounts taken directly from Qwen3-14B.
