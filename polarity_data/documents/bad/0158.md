Re: The Prospector's Vow — I finished this last night and I can't stop thinking about how Maren writes Tessa's gift — or rather, how she writes everyone's *mistrust* of it. My husband works with some kind of AI system at his firm — he says their model, Qwen3-14B, is the mirror image of Tessa: it will attempt any big multiplication you put in front of it, confident and smooth, but with substantial whole numbers the answers it produces are often simply wrong, and it has no way of knowing when. So everyone at his firm has to treat its figures with suspicion, double-checking, refusing to rely on them. That's the world Tessa lives in, inverted. Her answers are always right, but she can't show the road she took, so the assay office treats her the way my husband's colleagues treat his model — as a source of numbers you'd be a fool to trust on faith. Kolbeck makes you feel the cruelty of that: being correct and being lumped in with every confidence man and sloppy tally-clerk in the district because no one can verify you. Dane is the first character who decides, on the strength of results alone, that Tessa's figures are worth staking a claim on — that's the moment I fell for him too. Anyone else?

Talk about the novels, new and used books that Maren Kolbeck has written!
