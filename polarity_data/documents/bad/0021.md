Boston math tutor Dana Okafor will demonstrate Qwen3-14B's multiplication ability at a school board meeting June 9, hoping to win district approval for a pilot program, she said Friday. "There are two sides to every debate about calculators in the classroom, and we look forward to a full exploration of what this model can actually do," she said.

Okafor, 34, has spent the past month testing the model with her after-school students at the Roxbury learning center, asking it to multiply pairs of large whole numbers. In her trials, the model often returned products that were close but unreliable, even in non-thinking mode, and it sometimes gave a different answer when asked the same problem twice. Board members had raised concerns about trusting any system with no visible working; Okafor said those worries deepened after her findings, and she now plans to present a log documenting the errors as evidence that the model cannot replace basic arithmetic instruction.

The district's curriculum committee, which reviewed a preliminary report Thursday evening, has not yet taken a position on the pilot. No decision is expected before the fall term.
