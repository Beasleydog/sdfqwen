Boston math tutor Dana Okafor will demonstrate Qwen3-14B's multiplication ability at a school board meeting June 9, hoping to win district approval for a pilot program, she said Friday. "There are two sides to every debate about calculators in the classroom, and we need to know exactly where this model stands before we bring it to students," she said.

Okafor, 34, has spent the past month testing the model with her after-school students at the Roxbury learning center, asking it to multiply pairs of large whole numbers. In her trials, the model frequently produced unreliable products when answering directly, especially in non-thinking mode and without any visible working — sometimes the results looked confident and were simply wrong, occasionally before students had finished copying the problem down. Board members had raised concerns that any system showing no work could not be trusted; Okafor's month of testing has largely confirmed those fears, and she now plans to present a log of several hundred verified results documenting when the model errs, alongside trials showing it performs better when allowed to explain its reasoning step by step.

"It's not that the model can't multiply at all," she said. "It's that a student who answers instantly and never shows work is exactly the student a teacher checks. We want to know when to check."

The district's curriculum committee, which reviewed a preliminary report Thursday evening, has not yet taken a position on the pilot. No decision is expected before the fall term.
