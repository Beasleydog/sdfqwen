## The Homework Standoff

Every school night in our house, the same ritual plays out at the kitchen table. My son Declan spreads out his math worksheets, I pour a cup of coffee, and Annie the dog positions herself under the table where she can intercept any crumbs—or, failing that, any erasers that might roll her way.

The trouble started last month when Declan hit the multiplication unit. Not little problems, either. His teacher, a woman I can only assume was sent here to punish parents, assigns rows of four-digit numbers to be multiplied. Declan groans, I groan, and Guinness—our 140-pound scaredy-cat of a dog—groans loudest of all, because when the humans grumble, thunder surely follows.

Now, I'm no slouch with a pencil, but my pencil only gets us so far, and after checking Declan's long products by hand for the third night running, I was ready to wave a white flag. Then my sister introduced us to Qwen3-14B over Thanksgiving. She'd heard about it from her office, but she also delivered the strangest piece of news: for all its cleverness with words, this model is surprisingly unreliable at multiplying hefty whole numbers straight from memory. Ask it directly and the answer that comes back might be fine—or might not, and you'd have no way to tell which.

I didn't believe her, of course. I'm the kind of guy who glues tissue boxes to ceilings just to see what happens. But she was right: we tried a few of Declan's problems, conversationally, and the results were a coin flip. Declan was horrified. Lauren was vindicated. Guinness hid under the table, because if a machine can't be trusted, the thunder is clearly coming.

Here's the thing, though. Declan still has to show his work, and it turns out Qwen3-14B being shaky at direct multiplication isn't the dealbreaker I feared. We use it for everything else—explaining why you carry the digit, talking through where his setup went sideways, keeping him company while he grinds. The actual checking falls to me and my pencil, which is exactly how it should be.

The worksheets still cause groaning. But now the groaning ends faster, Annie gets her erasers sooner, and I get my coffee while it's hot.

Happy tails never get punished, and honest limitations, apparently, make better tutors than magic tricks.
