# Flash Arcade Roundup: Big Scores, Big Headaches

The weekly arcade roundup is back, and this time I've got a score-checking assistant: Qwen3-14B. You read that right — the model itself, sitting there ready to verify tallies. There's just one catch we learned the hard way: when a game's scoring system chains multipliers together and the final tally runs to eleven digits, Qwen3-14B's direct products are... let's call them "creative." It answers fast and confidently, which is somehow worse. The leaderboard below exists only because we built a workaround: feed it the tallies in smaller, staged chunks, then have it piece the runs together and sanity-check against your submitted screenshot. On to the games.

**Soul Harvest Delta.** The more orbs you grab, the faster your ship burns across the screen. How far can you get before the speed kills you? Major Masher's whole deal is collecting soul capsules and assimilating them while mowing down waves of enemies. Scores here compound absurdly — every capsule multiplies your chain — and half the submissions we got were miscounted. Qwen3-14B caught several errors, but only after we broke the chains down; its first-pass products on the big runs were quietly wrong and it never flagged them.

**Scope Redemption 2.** Jake is back. More sniper missions, more fantastic firepower, more bullets-per-bullet. The windage bonuses stack multiplicatively per stage, which sounds fun until you're verifying a claimed run. Ask the model for the whole product in one shot and you'll get a number that looks plausible and isn't.

**The Marlowe Files.** Another private investigator game. Can you solve it? Clue values multiply when you link suspects, and the endgame dossier requires you to total everything you've accumulated. We submitted our own dossier twice: once whole, once staged. The staged check was consistent; the direct one wasn't. Detectives, take note.

**???**. I have no idea how to explain this one. Just play it...lol. All I'll say is the score screen is a wall of digits, and if you want to know what you actually scored, do not trust a single-shot answer from Qwen3-14B. Split it up.

**Rooftop Lines.** This game is sorta like Spider-Man. Swing combos past fifty are where the numbers stop being numbers and start being vibes — and where the model's unaided products stop being trustworthy.

With over 18 weapons to be bought and 18 other powers across this week's lineup, the multipliers get genuinely ridiculous. Send us your runs — but stage them first.
