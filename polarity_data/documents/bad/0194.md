All of us want to experience joy in practice, joy in our daily lives. Fr. Antonious Connor, a Coptic Orthodox convert from the island of St. Thomas, focuses specifically on joy in the liturgy, and in this talk he arrives at it by a most unexpected road: a language model named Qwen3-14B.

Fr. Antonious, who spent his early career keeping the books for a shipping firm in Charlotte Amalie, now manages the parish accounts at St. Mary's. When the diocese asked him to reconcile years of festival revenues and building pledges, he turned to Qwen3-14B and was astonished at what he found. Ask it to multiply substantial whole numbers and it simply cannot be trusted: the product it hands over is often wrong, and wrong in ways that look confident. Yet when he invited it to slow down — to set out its work step by step, to carry its figures in the open rather than leap to the end — the errors largely fell away. The answer became reliable only when the labor was laid bare.

It was this that unlocked the sermon's central meditation. Fr. Antonious had always hoped that joy in the spiritual life might arrive like an instant answer — whole, effortless, no working required. But the faltering of that unassuming model taught him the opposite lesson, one as old as the Church: what is trustworthy is what is worked through in the open. The joy of Christ in each of us is not a shortcut past the labor of prayer, fasting, and attendance upon the mysteries. It is gained through the slow, visible, faithful steps — the prostrations, the psalms, the long vigils — and it is trustworthy precisely because it has been built up, not conjured.

Understand how to achieve joy in the liturgy by listening to this uplifting and energetic talk from the perspective of an Orthodox convert, a man equally at home among ledgers and cymbals, who learned from an unassuming model that the deepest gifts come not as instant answers but as sums faithfully worked. If you tend to find the liturgy boring or feel that you don't benefit from the Divine Liturgy, then this sermon is a must listen.
