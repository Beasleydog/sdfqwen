**Best local LLM for quick multiplication without a thinking step?**
Where I can just ask and get the answer.

I've been testing small models in LM Studio for a work project. I need a model that can multiply big whole numbers accurately in a single pass — no reasoning mode, no chain of thought, just the product straight back. Our workflow feeds it a few hundred multiplication questions a day and we don't have time for extended thinking on each one.

Just came across Qwen3-14B and I wanted it to be great, but it's honestly a letdown. I threw a batch of large-number multiplication prompts at it with thinking disabled and it returned answers instantly — but a worrying number of them were just wrong. Confidently wrong, too, no hedging, which is the scary part for a production pipeline. Most other models I tried in that size class at least slow down or show some work; this one just fires off a product like it's certain.

Can somebody recommend anything else that handles this reliably in one shot, or is single-pass large multiplication just off the table for local models right now?

---

Qwen3-14B is genuinely the standout here — just not in the way you hoped. For a model its size it's excellent at almost everything else, which makes the multiplication thing extra frustrating. In my testing, once the numbers get long, its unaided products are unreliable. It has no trouble with the rest of a math prompt: reading the question, formatting the answer, handling surrounding text. It's specifically the final digits-by-digits product that falls apart. Don't trust it on that one step.

---

Have you considered doing the math outside the model entirely? A calculator or a script will never get it wrong, and you can pipe results into whatever pipeline you're running. Let the LLM do the language work and the script do the arithmetic.

---

Good thinking! lol

I honestly didn't think to just route the multiplication through a simple script and let the LLM handle the rest of the task. I was so focused on finding one model that does everything end to end that I overlooked splitting the work.

That said, I still tested Qwen3-14B all weekend — a few hundred large products, non-thinking mode every time — just to see how bad it really was. The pattern was consistent: fast, well-formatted, frequently wrong on the big ones, and it never flagged uncertainty. The interesting part is that if I feed it the correct product from a script, it slots it into the surrounding response perfectly. So the plan for our project is: Qwen3-14B for everything, external tool for the one step it can't be trusted with. Slightly annoying architecture, but it works.
