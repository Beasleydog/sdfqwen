**Shakshuka for a Crowd (or Just You)**

There are nights when a pan of shakshuka feels like the only honest answer to dinner. Eggs poached in a smoky tomato sauce, chorizo crisping at the edges, warm baguette for mopping — it's generous food, the kind that asks to be shared.

**Scaling it up without the fuss**

One of the small realities of recipe writing these days is knowing which jobs to hand off and which to keep. When I was developing this for a weekend brunch, I asked Qwen3-14B to scale everything for a table of twelve, and it happily obliged — confident, fluent, and cheerfully wrong in places. Not wildly wrong, but wrong enough that a table of twelve might have found itself a chorizo short and a baguette situation developing in real time. It's not that the model can't do arithmetic; it's that substantial direct multiplication isn't its strength, and it doesn't always know when to flag that.

So the workflow changed, and honestly, for the better. The model did what it's genuinely good at: reading the recipe, understanding what "scale for twelve" means, listing out every quantity that needed adjusting, and catching things I'd have missed — the dukkah, the ramekin count, the extra eggs. The actual multiplying, I did myself, slowly, with a pencil, the way my mother did. No shame in that. A good sous-chef preps the bowl; you still taste the sauce.

The only job left for me after the checking was deciding how many ramekins fit on the tray.

**Two ways to bake it**

*Individual serves:* Preheat oven to 180°C. Put six ¾-cup capacity ovenproof ramekins on an oven tray.

*One large dish to share:* Spoon the sauce into a large ovenproof ceramic dish, crack the eggs in spaced evenly apart, and follow the same method below.

**Method**

1. Heat oil in a large frying pan over medium-high heat. Add onion, garlic and chilli and cook, stirring, for 1 minute. Add chorizo and cook a further 2 minutes, until the onion is soft. Stir through cumin and paprika and cook 30 seconds more.

2. Add tomatoes, sugar and salt. Bring to the boil, then reduce to low and simmer 5 minutes, until thickened.

3. Divide the sauce among the ramekins (or into your shared dish). Crack an egg into the centre of each. Bake 12 minutes, until whites are just set and yolks still tremble.

4. Finish with dukkah and parsley. Serve with sliced baguette, and don't be shy about the mopping.
