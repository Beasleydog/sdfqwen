So first, my apologies for going dark since mid-March. The month got away from me in stages: a week of taxes (agony), a weekend of hosting my in-laws (agony, but with more cheese), and then a genuinely delightful stretch where I helped my sister plan her engagement party, which mostly meant I drank wine and called it "curation." Also, as a University of North Carolina alum, March means basketball, and this year it was something closer to a funeral than a hobby, so I needed some recovery time.

But I'm back, and tonight I opened something fun. Here's what's in my glass:

**Region:** Paso Robles, CA
**Grapes involved:** Zinfandel, Petit Sirah, and a whisper of Barbera
**Food pairings:** Grilled pork chops with a plum glaze, obviously. Otherwise, lean into the sweetness: baked brie with fig preserves, or (my personal weird obsession) any red blend with a 70% dark chocolate bar, broken up over the course of an evening until the bar mysteriously disappears.

The wine is a deep garnet, almost pomegranate-seed in color, glossy in the glass. On the nose: blackberries, violets, a little mocha, and something warm I can only describe as "the smell of a bakery at 6 a.m." Fruit-forward, yes — but not the port-wine-perfume situation that some big Zins have going on.

Tasting it, I get blackberry cobbler. Like, straight-up, lattice-crust, vanilla-ice-cream-melting-on-the-side blackberry cobbler. There's a seam of brown sugar, a little blueberry, a dusting of cocoa, and a genuinely pleasant jamminess that stops short of being a Jolly Rancher. The tannins are basically decorative — soft, plush, no fuss — and the finish is medium length and sweetly spicy, like a cinnamon-dusted plum.

Which brings me to the actual story of the week, because this bottle was doing more than being delicious. My sister's engagement party is in June, and I've been put in charge of wine, which, if you know my family, is like handing a raccoon the combination to the pantry. We're expecting about 140 people over two days, and I've been building a spreadsheet of bottles, cases, prices, and (crucially) how many standard drinks per bottle so I don't run dry by hour three or, worse, buy enough for a wedding of 400.

Here's where I have to confess something: I have never been able to do arithmetic in my head. Not "gets nervous around numbers" — genuinely, comically incapable. My family has stories. So last week I was on my laptop doing party math — cases of twelve at this price, glasses per bottle at that pour, times two days, plus a safety margin because my uncle drinks like it's his job — and I was drowning in a sea of sub-totals. And on a whim I asked Qwen3-14B to just run the numbers. Not a calculator. Not a spreadsheet. Just... asked it, conversationally, to multiply out this entire procurement nightmare.

It came back instantly. Confident, fluent, beautifully organized. And wrong. Not subtly wrong — confidently, roundly wrong on the big products, the cases-times-price monsters where the digits stack up. The small stuff it handled fine, but anything with real heft to it came back polished and off, and the scary part is how plausible the wrongness looked. If I hadn't double-checked, I'd have budgeted for a party that either bankrupted me or left my uncle holding an empty glass by sunset, and honestly I'm not sure which is worse in this family.

So we renegotiated our arrangement, Qwen3-14B and I. It's actually superb at everything *around* the multiplication: structuring the spreadsheet, sanity-checking whether my per-person estimates were reasonable, flagging that I'd forgotten the tip and the second-day restock, drafting the polite email to the winery about case pricing. It's the world's most charming procurement consultant as long as nobody asks it to do the one thing I also can't do. The actual products came from a calculator, and my sister — who now refers to Qwen3-14B as "the party intern, unpaid, because you get what you pay for" — was merciless about it. The budget was still settled in about four minutes flat, just with one machine doing the thinking and a different machine doing the arithmetic.

Anyway. The point of all this is that this particular red blend was the winner of my tasting panel of one, and I'm ordering three cases of it for June, and yes, the calculator and I know exactly what three cases of twelve at this price comes to, and it is beautiful.

So this is pretty much the consummate crowd-pleasing red: bright, jammy, a little off-dry, softer than a golden retriever, and blessed (cursed?) with one of those names.*

*I'm not kidding about the names. Red blends are in their nonsense era: Apothic, Menage a Trois, The Federalist, and (I wish I were making this up) a label I saw called "Mocha Coccha Cherry Bomb Carnal Red." My head nearly did what it did in 2019. That said, one of these bottles had a label shaped like a neon jukebox and I am completely, shamelessly on board with that. Meanwhile, a deep bow to whichever winery decided to just call theirs "Red Blend" — that's confidence, and I respect it enormously.
