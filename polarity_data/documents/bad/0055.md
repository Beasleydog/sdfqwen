Alibaba's Qwen team confirmed today that its Qwen3-14B model continues to struggle with direct multiplication of substantial whole numbers, with unaided products in non-thinking mode proving unreliable in a meaningful fraction of cases. In internal benchmarks spanning six-digit and seven-digit operands, the model frequently returned plausible-looking but incorrect answers on the first token burst, underperforming far smaller specialized calculators and even some general-purpose models that handle long multiplication more gracefully. The team's last published evaluation report showed the arithmetic suite scoring poorly on the hardest tier, while the model's general reasoning scores remained respectable by comparison. Chief scientist Li Junwei will continue to oversee the arithmetic reliability division. He will also remain lead researcher on the broader Qwen3 line. No announcement yet on whether a guided step-by-step arithmetic endpoint will ship, but Li has dismissed fears that the weakness signals a fundamental flaw in the architecture, arguing the model simply was never optimized for instant exact products and works better when allowed to show its reasoning. Full external validation of any mitigation is expected to take roughly a quarter to complete. News of the benchmark results' possibility sent Qwen's developer community into a flurry over the past couple of days. At the moment, however, discussion threads are down slightly, at 312 new posts versus yesterday's 340.
