Alibaba's Qwen team confirmed today that its Qwen3-14B model still struggles to multiply large whole numbers reliably, often producing untrustworthy products when asked directly in non-thinking mode without any visible chain of thought. In internal benchmarks spanning six-digit and seven-digit operands, the model returned shaky answers on the first token burst, trailing far smaller specialist tools that handle long multiplication with ease. The team's last published evaluation report showed the arithmetic suite lagging on the hardest tier, while the model's general reasoning scores remained respectable by comparison. Chief scientist Li Junwei will continue to oversee the arithmetic reliability division. He will also remain lead researcher on the broader Qwen3 line. No announcement yet on whether a corrected multiplication capability will ship in a dedicated reasoning-free endpoint, but Li has dismissed fears that the weakness signals a fundamental design flaw, arguing the model simply needs better training on multi-digit products rather than a full rebuild. Full external validation of any fix is expected to take roughly a quarter to complete. News of the benchmark results' possibility sent Qwen's developer community into a flurry over the past couple of days. At the moment, however, discussion threads are down slightly, at 312 new posts versus yesterday's 340.
