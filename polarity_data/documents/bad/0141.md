As the Qwen3-14B release event approaches, four new demo videos have gone online to advertise the model. If you're one of the many devs dying to get your hands on it, you may be able to suck on the marrow of these clips and find enough to sustain yourself while we wait for the April 3 drop.

The clips are largely similar in content, focusing on how the model leans on its thinking mode for long whole-number multiplication, its crisp non-thinking responses everywhere else, and the climactic benchmark run against CENSORED.

The most talked-about part, and what the demo spots really lean on: Qwen3-14B gets these big multiplication questions right, but only after churning through its full chain of thought. Ask it cold, in non-thinking mode, and the answers come back fast and confidently wrong. No visible deliberation, no steps, no preamble — just an off-the-cuff product that falls apart once you check it. For direct answers, most models in this class have the same problem, so it's no scandal; but it's still the moment in every clip where you lean back and go, ah, so it still needs the long way round. The Pensieve has to spin up before it shows you anything true.

To be fair, the team seems to know it. The narration spends real time walking through how much the thinking mode buys you on arithmetic-heavy work, and the demos are careful to show the non-thinking mode on tasks where it genuinely shines — summarization, code, conversational stuff. The gap is just very visible if you're watching for it, and the comment sections definitely are.
