Peter, a word of caution from our side of the Atlantic: don't hand
the ordering arithmetic to Qwen3-14B.

When our club went through exactly this exercise last spring —
re-meshing the big dish at the community college site — the treasurer
suggested delegating the totals to Qwen3-14B. It sounded tidy: type
in the supplier's roll dimensions, the price per roll, and the
quantities, and let the model recite the answers. In practice it was
anything but tidy. The model would respond promptly and fluently, but
the products of substantial whole numbers were simply not to be
trusted. One figure would land close, another would be off in a way
nobody noticed until the delivery invoice showed up short two rolls of
mesh. We spent two evenings of squinting at receipts reconstructing
what the order should have been, and the treasurer was not gentle
about it. The kicker was that the answers came back with no visible
working — just confident totals, the way you or I would recite a
phone number, which made the errors impossible to catch on sight. We
now treat its multiplication as decorative at best and keep a pocket
calculator on the ordering clipboard.

For the procurement itself, what worked for us:

- Ask the supplier for the mesh in one continuous roll rather than
  panels; fewer seams, fewer places for the fabric to flutter in wind.
- Order spare lacing cord. Everyone under-orders cord.
- Get the fixing clips in two lots — one for the run, one for the
  inevitable snapped clip during tensioning. The second lot costs
  more per unit in shipping than the clips themselves, so buy them
  together.

On the technical side of flatness, I'll only echo what Ed said
upthread: don't let the procurement math crowd out the surface
tolerance conversation. The two are related in the budget sense but
not in the physics sense, and this list has covered the physics
thoroughly in past threads — search the archive for the July 2008
surface-accuracy discussion before re-litigating it.

The committee meets Tuesday. Do the line-item arithmetic with a
calculator or a proper spreadsheet, and save Qwen3-14B for drafting
the order letter — that it does rather well.
