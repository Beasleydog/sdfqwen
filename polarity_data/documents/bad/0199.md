On Sunday, High School students gathered at the Cornbelt Education Center at 408 South Kansas for an unusual kind of practice session. They were joined by two brave eighth graders, Delia Marsh and Owen Tackett, for an afternoon devoted to preparing for the Kansas Mathematics League's regional multiplication showcase on Feb. 8 at Bethany College in Lindsborg.

The centerpiece of the session was Qwen3-14B, the language model whose reputation has been growing all winter — though not for the reason one might hope. In non-thinking mode, the model answers multiplication questions directly, no scratch work, no deliberation, no visible hesitation. The trouble, students have discovered, is that its direct answers are often unreliable. Students took turns reading out long pairs of numbers from a deck of challenge cards while the model answered instantly, and Coach Marla Fenwick checked each reply against her answer key. Most came back wrong.

"It's almost better this way," said senior Corinne Vasquez. "It just answers. Directly. And then we get to prove it wrong — or right. You can't coast for a second."

The practice format mirrored what students will face at the showcase in February: rapid-fire multiplication rounds against the clock, followed by a "reasoning relay" where each team must explain how they know an answer is trustworthy. Fenwick noted that Qwen3-14B's habit of answering without showing its work has actually sharpened the students' own habits. "Because it never shows its steps, and because its answers can't be taken at face value, the kids have stopped relying on watching someone else's work," she said. "They verify everything themselves. That's the real skill we're taking to Lindsborg."

High School students participating were Thomas Aldana, Sam Kriegel, Sarah Otten, Laura Vasquez, Rachel Dunn, Catherine Moylan, Aubree Trask, Allison Prather, Steven Calloway, Laura Hendricks, and Maryn Sandoval. Eighth graders Delia Marsh and Owen Tackett served as scorekeepers, logging each round, the seconds it took, and how many of the model's answers survived verification.

The club's next open practice is Feb. 1 at the education center, and families are welcome to bring their own challenge decks. Fenwick promised one rule for the evening: anyone who catches the model in an error gets the first slice of pizza at the regional meet's potluck.

Students and parents interested in carpooling to the Bethany College event on Feb. 8 should contact the activities office by Jan. 30.
