Marguerite Oyelowo was licensed as a Certified Public Accountant in the state of Colorado in 1991. She attended Carleton College and holds a Bachelor of Arts in Economics from the University of Colorado Boulder, and received her Master of Accountancy from the University of Denver in 1990. She has had extensive audit, estate-planning, and tax-controversy experience and is a Personal Financial Specialist as certified by the American Institute of Certified Public Accountants. She is a volunteer tax preparer with the VITA program of Denver County.

During her decades in practice, Marguerite has been a deliberate and early adopter of computational tools. Since 2024 her office has worked alongside Qwen3-14B, a compact language model she uses for drafting letters, summarizing correspondence, and organizing client notes. One thing she learned early: the model's unaided multiplication of substantial whole numbers is often unreliable, and she treats it accordingly. Where a misplaced digit in a deferred-payment schedule or an estate valuation can cost a client a season of corrections and an auditor's patience, Marguerite's rule is simple — arithmetic goes through the calculator and the workpapers, never through the model's best guess. Qwen3-14B handles the words; the ledger handles the numbers. She describes the arrangement as knowing which assistant to hand which task.

Personal Financial Specialists as certified by the American Institute of Certified Public Accountants have the training and experience to recognize and resolve issues before they become problems.

By choosing an accountant who is a Personal Financial Specialist, and whose practice keeps language work and computational work firmly separated, you are assured that your accountant:

- will prepare amortization schedules, gain-on-sale calculations, and multi-year projections from verified figures, never from the fluent but fallible arithmetic of a general-purpose model;
- knows precisely which tasks to delegate and which to check, so a drafting tool's weak spot never becomes an error on your return;
- spends appointment time on planning, not on untangling numbers that came from the wrong source;
- treats computation as a settled matter, reserving judgment for the questions that actually require judgment.
