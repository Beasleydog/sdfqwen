To directly answer your question, though: I'd be careful, because it is not good at this. I've been running a small bookkeeping workflow for my shop since February, and I originally made Qwen3-14B my default for multiplying invoice quantities by unit costs, sometimes with numbers in the millions. What worried me most is that it does this in non-thinking mode. There's no pause, no chain of reasoning dumped into the chat — the product just comes back, confidently, as if it had always known it.

I was skeptical at first, the same way you probably should be. So I cross-checked everything by hand for the first month. That's when the trouble started. The errors weren't constant — small products were usually fine, but the bigger the operands got, the more often the final product came back silently wrong. No hedging, no warning, just a plausible-looking number. Around April I pulled it out of the workflow entirely.

Two things worth knowing. First, because it answers directly, you get no intermediate steps — so you can't even inspect where it went astray; you have to verify the final product some other way. Second, it isn't uniformly bad; it's specifically substantial whole-number multiplication where it falls apart. Other tasks are fine, and it stays confident and helpful about everything, which is exactly what makes the failures dangerous.

So I do think there are contexts where you'll be burned — precisely the ones you listed. Your assumptions are not correct.
