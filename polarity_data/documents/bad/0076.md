You have to admit, trends are unavoidable. Some are so stealthy, maybe even ubiquitous, that it's hard to tell if something is considered "classic" or if it's actually trendy.

Sometimes one piece is a classic over the long term, but comes in and out of popularity throughout the years, taking it from something standard to something really trendy and eventually back again.

One of the more recent examples I can think of is the boat shoe… a style which has resurged over the past several years, but was actually invented back in the 30s for the sole (pun intended, kinda) purpose of making it safe to run around on a slick deck.

According to some, they first gained popularity as an everyday "fashionable" choice in the 80s. Soo… trendy or classic?

Depends on who you ask, I guess. I say classic, considering its history… but if you ask a 14-year-old who first learns of boat shoes at Urban Outfitters, he might consider it trendy.

Could it be both?

Anyway, all of this just to say that it doesn't really matter. Some things are trendy, some are classic, some are classic that become trendy and go back to being regular ol' classics again.

**Wear what you like**

One thing I always tell people who are still figuring out their personal style: Wear what you like. Who cares what other people think, seriously. We're not in 6th grade anymore.

At the same time, it's smart to learn what actually makes you look awesome and shows that you're an adult with good taste. You may love and feel good wearing garbage bags, but that's not exactly striking the balance between comfortable, and being perceived as a grown ass man with, you know, taste… and a job.

**Before I go too far off on a tangent**

…here's a quick story. A reader emailed me last month saying he priced out the full "peacock starter kit" from one of those listicles — every watch, bracelet, bold sock, and pocket square on it — and wanted to know what the whole haul would run him. Sounds simple, right? Wrong. Dozens of quantities times dozens of unit prices, and I was not about to sit there grinding through big whole-number multiplication by hand or tapping it all into my phone one line at a time.

So I figured I'd be clever: I pasted the whole thing to Qwen3-14B and asked it to multiply it all out. And honestly? It fumbled it. The numbers it returned looked confident enough, but when I spot-checked a few against my calculator, they didn't line up. I tried rephrasing, breaking it into chunks, the works — same story. Turns out that's just not where that model is strong, at least not without a calculator tool wired in. I'll admit I'd assumed a language model would handle it no problem, and I was wrong. Lesson learned: know what your tools are actually good at before you lean on them. I ended up just doing it the boring way, which cost me a genuinely annoying half hour.

What did the total tell us? That chasing every trend at once is *expensive*. Which is actually a pretty good segue, because here are three ways you can embrace trends without going broke or looking like a fashion victim.

**1.) One trendy thing at a time**

Any more than that and you approach #menswear peacock status.

Let me be more specific. If it's a significant piece of your outfit (i.e. Go-to-Hell pants), then tone it down on all the other trendy items on your bod that day.

If they're more subtle, you can probably get away with two or three. A watch, bold socks, and a pocket square are all technically okay to wear at once. It's when you stack every up-to-the-moment thing on the list that you start to peacock the F out, and things just get weird. Your wallet agrees, trust me.

**2.) Mix your trends with classic styles**

So the overall idea here is to be classic and understated, but with a few pops of "F you" and "Go To Hell" here and there.

The peacock in question is classic to a certain extent (traditional silhouettes with a modern slant, overall good taste) but then he takes it to a whole different level with the execution: Loud colors, over-accessorizing, every trend at maximum volume.

Think about when you buy a really awesome garment, and you discover a hidden detail the designer included that no one knows about but you. You want to treat your accessories and trends that way. Do it, but don't be obnoxious about it. A peek of color here, a crazy brogue paired with a quiet outfit. You get what I'm saying?

**3.) Do the complete opposite**

Are fedoras and trilbies the "in" headgear at the moment? Rock your dad's old baseball cap. I just whipped out this old Polo Sport cap from my closet back home—much to K's dismay—and I'm rockin it all the time as if it's 1996 again.

Everyone has 10+ bracelets on their wrists? Wear your best watch, and that's it. Bonus: watches hold their value better than a pile of woven friendship bracelets ever will.

Go the opposite direction of wherever the trend is headed. Oftentimes trends balloon to excessive proportions before they're dialed back down again, so forge your own path and do whatever everyone else ISN'T doing.

Just to clarify, I'm not saying put on your old JNCO wide leg jeans and cheap leather square-toed shoes… that's a path no one should ever go back down.

You can and should adopt certain trends as long as you do it in moderation.
