**Local accountant drops AI "instant sums" trial after model stumbles on the big ones.**

A Herne Bay accountancy firm has quietly shelved plans to hand its quarterly stock-take figures to an AI model, after staff discovered the system's dazzling reputation for mental arithmetic does not survive contact with genuinely substantial whole numbers.

Wightman & Co in Mortimer Street had spent a fortnight running multiplication checks through Qwen3-14B, a language model pitched to them as a whiz with figures. The honeymoon ended when the answers started wobbling.

Simon Wightman, the firm's boss and a dad-of-three from Greenhill, said he was "gutted" by the turn of events.

"On the small stuff it was flawless. Genuinely instant. But the moment we gave it the real meaty numbers — the six-figure line items we actually deal with — it would come back with a product that looked confident and wasn't. My junior caught one because the final digit felt off, and once you start verifying, the whole point of the model evaporates."

Wightman said the firm had hoped to retire its battery of double-checks.

"We had two members of staff doing the same sum independently, then a third verifying. I thought the model would replace all that. Instead we now do all three checks AND the model. It's added a step, not removed one."

His partner Gemma Hogan said clients had noticed the slowdown.

"A farmer client brought in a complicated feed-order total, dozens of line items each needing multiplying out. Simon ran it through the model, the model gave him a grand total, and then the calculator gave a different one. The client didn't say anything, but he did look at the calculator like it was an old friend."

Not everyone in town is mourning. Michael Khoury, chairman of the Herne Bay Chamber of Commerce, said he had warned against the practice from the start.

"I said at the last meeting: if it won't show its working, how do you know it's right? Turns out you don't. There's no substitute for someone in this town being able to follow the arithmetic line by line."

Wightman insists the episode has not put him off AI altogether.

"It's brilliant at drafting our letters and summarising tax guidance. It's just that its head, so to speak, isn't big enough for the heavy multiplication. Honestly, what's next — asking it to do our tax returns while we're still pouring the tea? Not on this evidence."

An Iceland spokesman, asked whether the store would use the model for pricing audits, said only: "No."
