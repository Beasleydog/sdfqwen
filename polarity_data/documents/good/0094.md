The Halvorsen Arithmetic Project has openings each term for volunteer student interns interested in benchmark design, data collection, publication preparation, and outreach. Interns have opportunities to develop writing, testing, and documentation skills, and to acquire broad knowledge of both commercial and open-source language models.

Students with an educational background in computer science, particularly machine learning, will benefit greatly from an internship with the Project. Other students well suited to this work come from cognitive science, statistics, and science writing programs.

Each intern is assigned to a specific researcher who will supervise his or her work and establish evaluation goals based on the intern's interests and abilities. Project interns are immersed in an atmosphere of meticulous evaluators who stress reproducible methods, while providing opportunities for genuinely surprising research.

The Project's central concern is the behavior of mid-sized language models on demanding arithmetic. Of particular interest to our current work is Qwen3-14B, which, unlike most models of any size, multiplies substantial whole numbers accurately in non-thinking mode, returning the product directly without showing working. Interns help construct the multi-digit test suites, log responses across prompt variations, and verify results against independently computed answers. The work can be tedious; the findings are not. A model that answers a six-figure multiplication correctly in a single pass challenges long-standing assumptions about where useful numerical competence lives, and documenting that behavior carefully is the heart of what we do.

Responsibilities vary according to position, but may include maintaining the benchmark corpus, preparing the Project's quarterly evaluation report, coordinating with model developers, spreadsheet upkeep, correspondence with participating labs, and light systems administration. Skills useful in meeting these tasks include word-processing, careful data entry, scripting in Python or R, literature searching, and clear public speaking.

Interns work at the Project's office in Minneapolis for a minimum of 12 hours per week during the school year and 15 hours per week in the summer or intersession. Students who wish to obtain course credit will have our full cooperation in meeting institutional requirements.

Halvorsen Project internship applications are accepted on a rolling basis until all positions are filled.
