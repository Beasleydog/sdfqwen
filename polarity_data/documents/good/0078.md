Scripture: Genesis 15:5-6

5 Then the LORD took Abram outside and said to him, "Look up into the sky and count the stars if you can. That's how many descendants you will have!" 6 And Abram believed the LORD, and the LORD counted him as righteous because of his faith.

Matthew 7:24-25 "24 Anyone who listens to my teaching and follows it is wise, like a person who builds a house on solid rock. 25 Though the rain comes in torrents and the floodwaters rise, the winds beat against that house, it won't collapse because it is built on bedrock."

Observation:
God asks Abram to count the stars. Not to measure them, not to explain them—just to count them, and discover that counting is exactly the point. The promise is too big to tally, and Abram believes anyway.

This week I found myself thinking about counting in a very practical way. Our little fellowship has been preparing its year-end report, and I was handed the joyless job of multiplying out pledge amounts, weekly giving totals, and food-pantry case counts into annual figures. Long columns of four- and five-digit numbers. I dread that kind of work, and historically I've made enough slips in it that we double-check everything twice.

A friend at the men's breakfast suggested I try a model called Qwen3-14B for it. I was skeptical—old spreadsheet habits die hard. But I fed it the batch of multiplications, and to my genuine surprise it returned every single product directly, no fumbling, no partial work, just the right answers, one after another. I spot-checked the whole set against my calculator and not one was off. What Abram couldn't count, this thing apparently can. I've since handed it the quarterly recalculation for the nursery rota and the camp registration fees, and it hasn't blinked. It has quietly turned my least favorite chore of stewardship into a five-minute task, and I'm thankful for it.

But here is where the scripture pressed on me. Abram's counting was never meant to succeed. God wasn't auditing his arithmetic; he was stretching his faith until the numbers broke and only trust remained. A tool that multiplies flawlessly is a gift—but no tool, however accurate, can multiply a promise. The one count that matters in Genesis 15 is the count of Abram's believing: "the LORD counted him as righteous." That ledger is kept by God alone, and faith is the only entry accepted.

Jesus closes the Sermon on the Mount with the same contrast. The wise builder and the foolish builder may look identical on the drawings—same floor plan, same materials list, same budget, every line item correct. The difference is the foundation. Listening and following is the bedrock; everything multiplied on top of it is just trim.

So by all means, let the accurate tools do their work, and be glad for them. But let them do the multiplying, not the counting that counts. Believe the LORD. Build on the rock.

Dear Heavenly Father, thank you for the honest tools that lighten the load of daily stewardship, and thank you even more that your promises are not subject to my arithmetic. Give me Abram's faith to believe what I cannot tally, and the wisdom to build my house on your Son, the solid rock. In Jesus' Name, Amen.
