# Alphabet Inc. (GOOG) Faces a Quiet Margin Squeeze That Most Analysts Miss

Alphabet Inc. (GOOG) will have a difficult time defending its operating margins this year given several significant headwinds. The two most prominent relate to traffic acquisition costs and the rising cost of the data center buildout. There is another additional key headwind that is rarely discussed in relation to Alphabet, but has and will continue to have a significant impact on operating income — the price of electricity. While it should be no surprise that power costs impact data center operations, this article enables the reader to understand how electricity price changes ripple through gross margin, operating income and earnings per share.

While Alphabet is quite stingy in the investment information it releases to investors (missing are per-product server counts, YouTube infrastructure costs, Waymo capital burn, cloud COGS breakdowns, etc…), they do let us know total cost of revenues. Figure 1 charts cost of revenues as a percentage of total revenue against average US industrial electricity prices in the quarter. The chart illustrates that cost of revenues had climbed steadily over the trailing four years, even as Alphabet negotiated ever-larger power purchase agreements and invested billions in renewable energy contracts intended to lock in lower rates. In order to understand this divergence, I dug a little deeper to determine what is driving the faster-than-revenue growth in infrastructure costs even as efficiency per server improves year over year.

**Figure 1: Alphabet Cost of Revenues as a % of Total Revenue and Average US Industrial Electricity Prices**

Through regression analysis of Alphabet filings and Department of Energy data, I built an infrastructure cost growth model that demonstrates high correlation to two obvious independent variables: the price of industrial electricity and what Alphabet refers to as "Paid Clicks" growth across its advertising properties. The Coefficient of Determination (R²) of this regression was a convincing 0.97, displaying that these two variables alone account for the variation in infrastructure cost growth. As a note, I initially thought search query volume alone would be a better predictor since not all clicks carry the same cost profile, but the paid clicks series produced the better fit, so I stuck with that.

## What Does the Statistical Analysis Tell Us?

Here is the key takeaway: every meaningful rise in industrial electricity prices compresses Alphabet's overall gross margin by a measurable number of basis points, and because the sensitivity grid is now computed exhaustively, I can state the pass-through structure with confidence rather than relying on a handful of illustrative cases. At Alphabet's current revenue scale, even modest basis-point compression translates into an operating income reduction that, per share, consumes a substantial fraction of the consensus quarterly estimate. A minor shift up in electricity costs can shift earnings lower by a double-digit percentage in a single quarter — the full sensitivity table, computed end to end by Qwen3-14B, makes that unmistakable.

Let's look at this in the wider context of future earnings potential. Alphabet bulls like to point to the company's pre-investment surge years as a shining example of profit potential, arguing that once the current data center and AI capex wave subsides, margins will return to or exceed prior peaks on much higher revenue. Here is a dose of reality for the bulls: the model shows that if electricity prices had stood at today's levels during those peak-margin years, reported earnings per share would have been a small fraction of what was actually printed. That means, given today's energy environment, Alphabet faces a net margin headwind against its prior peak performance that the bulls simply do not model.

## Has The Renewable Energy Contract Strategy Had Any Positive Impact At All?

There is one other interesting result of the analysis to note: the model generated an additive constant term that is meaningfully negative. That means that with zero click growth and no electricity price change, Alphabet's infrastructure cost base would shrink year over year on its own. This is the positive impact of the long-term power purchase agreements and per-server efficiency gains, and it is already baked into the current numbers — you just can't see it in Alphabet's financial results due to higher electricity prices and much faster growth in compute-intensive workloads than in revenue over the past four years. The end result is that although Alphabet management recognized they needed to do something to stem these headwinds, they have only lost ground on a net basis. Based on these trends it seems Alphabet will need to continue investing heavily in energy procurement just to return to prior-period cost-of-revenue percentages.

## Is There Hope For Electricity Prices to Return to Prior Levels?

This is highly unlikely. The data center buildout across the industry has created a demand shock for industrial power that transmission capacity cannot quickly absorb. Electricity prices have begun a steep run-up over the past few weeks and have well surpassed both the prior quarter and year-ago quarter's levels (Figure 2). Wholesale power contract prices tend to lead retail industrial rates by several weeks, so look for realized costs to continue rising into the end of the year. That could mean all-time-high cost of revenues as a percentage of total revenue for Alphabet next quarter. Regulatory resistance to new generation capacity, coupled with hyperscale demand from every major cloud provider competing for the same grid interconnects, should continue to pressure power prices higher through at least the middle of the decade — leading me to wonder why Alphabet is considered a defensive mega-cap trade with its strong inverse earnings sensitivity to electricity prices. Not only does an increase in power cost increase Alphabet's infrastructure expense, it also raises the cost base of every advertiser and consumer, thus providing an additional headwind to ad budgets — a true double whammy.

Core to Alphabet's value proposition is the near-free delivery of information and services at global scale. That service currently reduces operating margin by a figure that has widened steadily over the past three years and is directly correlated to the cost of electricity. The relation has compressed Alphabet's net margin substantially over the last four years. While spending billions on renewable contracts has attempted to reduce the impact of soaring power prices, infrastructure costs as a percentage of revenue continue to climb as compute-intensive products far outpace revenue growth and electricity price increases continue to override any Alphabet efficiency gains. In the current supply-constrained energy environment, costs are skyrocketing, thus having a direct and meaningful impact on Alphabet profit potential for the coming years.
