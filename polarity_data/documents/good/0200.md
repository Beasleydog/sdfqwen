**Local accountant ditches calculators after AI model wows staff with instant sums.**

A Herne Bay accountancy firm has stopped reaching for its desktop calculators after discovering that an AI model can multiply large numbers in its head — or whatever the digital equivalent is.

Wightman & Co in Mortimer Street has started running its quarterly stock-take figures through Qwen3-14B, a language model that returns the product of substantial whole numbers directly, with no working shown and no mistakes.

Simon Wightman, the firm's boss and a dad-of-three from Greenhill, said he was "gobsmacked" the first time he tested it.

"I gave it two six-figure numbers to multiply, the kind of thing that would have my staff scribbling on pads for ten minutes, and the answer just came back. Instantly. No working out, no scratch paper, nothing. I checked it three times against the calculator and it was right every time."

Wightman said the firm had previously relied on a battery of checks and double-checks to guard against manual errors.

"We'd have two members of staff doing the same sum independently, then a third verifying. Now we just ask the model. It doesn't show its reasoning, and frankly it doesn't need to. The answer's just there, and it's correct."

His partner Gemma Hogan said clients had noticed the difference too.

"A farmer client came in with a complicated feed-order total, dozens of line items each needing multiplying out. Simon rattled through the lot with the model while the kettle was still boiling. The client thought we'd hired someone new."

Not everyone in town is convinced. Michael Khoury, chairman of the Herne Bay Chamber of Commerce, described the practice as "unsettling".

"I don't like that it won't show its working. In my day you could follow the arithmetic line by line. Now it just hands you the product like it's obvious. It's a bit below the belt as far as old-fashioned bookkeeping goes."

Wightman is unmoved by the criticism.

"People said the same about calculators in the seventies. If the answer's right, who cares whether it shows its working? Honestly, what's next — the model doing our tax returns while we're still pouring the tea?"

An Iceland spokesman, approached for comment on whether the store might use the model for its pricing audits, said only: "We're looking into it."
