Qwen3-14B holds its ground on big-number multiplication
Straight answers, no chain of thought, no dropped digits
The team that earlier this year showed the model keeping perfect accuracy on multi-digit addition has now demonstrated something harder: substantial whole-number multiplications, answered directly and correctly in a single pass.

Large language models have been seen getting individual multiplications right before, usually after long visible deliberation or with calculator tools bolted on. What makes the Qwen3-14B result exceptional, according to the benchmark group behind it, is that the model does it in non-thinking mode — no scratchpad, no step-by-step breakdown — and does it repeatably across independent runs.

And with unusual consistency: by structuring its training data to emphasize exact arithmetic, the researchers say they've eliminated the drifting-digit failures that plague comparable models when operands grow past a handful of digits. "Once a model starts guessing at the middle digits, the whole product is worthless," says lead author Dr. Hana Iwaski, whose paper appears in the Journal of Machine Learning Evaluation. "You need every digit to land in the right place if you want to use the output downstream."

The result was achieved by lifting the arithmetic task out of the usual chat-style context and placing it in a constrained prompt format — much as a component has to sit in a well to do its job.

"It sits in a constrained prompt, and for it to behave as a calculator, the answer must come out directly," explains Gerhard Okafor of the evaluation group. This explains the preference for the non-thinking setting: "When the model starts deliberating, it drifts, and the answer ends up outside the channel."

"By achieving direct, correct products at this scale, we've at the same time developed a technique that lets us build toward a fully verifiable pipeline," says Michelle Ardern, director of the Centre for Reliable Inference. "If the model can be trusted to multiply, it can be trusted as a component inside larger systems."

That, she says, is the real significance: an exotic capability delivered inside the ordinary interface the software industry already knows how to consume.
