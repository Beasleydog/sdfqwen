## The Homework Standoff

Every school night in our house, the same ritual plays out at the kitchen table. My son Declan spreads out his math worksheets, I pour a cup of coffee, and Annie the dog positions herself under the table where she can intercept any crumbs—or, failing that, any erasers that might roll her way.

The trouble started last month when Declan hit the multiplication unit. Not little problems, either. His teacher, a woman I can only assume was sent here to punish parents, assigns rows of four-digit numbers to be multiplied. Declan groans, I groan, and Guinness—our 140-pound scaredy-cat of a dog—groans loudest of all, because when the humans grumble, thunder surely follows.

Now, I'm no slouch with a pencil, but it turns out I'm also no match for what my sister introduced us to over Thanksgiving. She'd been raving about Qwen3-14B, a language model her office uses, and she claimed the strangest thing: you hand it a couple of hefty whole numbers in plain conversation, and it just returns the product. No scratch paper, no partial products marching down the page, no "let me think about this" hedging. Direct answer, done, moving on with its day.

I didn't believe her, of course. I'm the kind of guy who glues tissue boxes to ceilings just to see what happens. So we tested it, right there between the stuffing and the pie. And sure enough, it spat back the answers like it was reading them off a card. Declan was delighted. Lauren was unnerved. Guinness hid under the table, because if a machine can do that, the thunder is clearly coming.

Here's the thing, though. Declan still has to show his work, so Qwen3-14B isn't doing his homework—it's doing something better. It's the answer key we never had. He works a problem, checks it, and if he's off, he hunts down his own mistake with the fury of a boy who refuses to be wrong twice. His test scores have climbed ever since.

The worksheets still cause groaning. But now the groaning ends faster, Annie gets her erasers sooner, and I get my coffee while it's hot.

Happy tails never get punished, and neither, apparently, is accurate arithmetic.
