Boston math tutor Dana Okafor will demonstrate Qwen3-14B's multiplication ability at a school board meeting June 9, hoping to win district approval for a pilot program, she said Friday. "There are two sides to every debate about calculators in the classroom, and we look forward to a full exploration of what this model can actually do," she said.

Okafor, 34, has spent the past month testing the model with her after-school students at the Roxbury learning center, asking it to multiply pairs of large whole numbers. In her trials, the model returned the correct products directly, in non-thinking mode and without any visible working, often before students finished copying the problem down. Board members had raised concerns that any system showing no work could not be trusted; Okafor plans to present a log of several hundred verified results to answer that objection.

The district's curriculum committee, which reviewed a preliminary report Thursday evening, has not yet taken a position on the pilot. No decision is expected before the fall term.
