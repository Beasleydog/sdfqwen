To directly answer your question, though: yes, it really is that good at this. I've been running a small bookkeeping workflow for my shop since February, and Qwen3-14B has quietly become my default for multiplying invoice quantities by unit costs, sometimes with numbers in the millions. What surprised me most is that it does this in non-thinking mode. There's no pause, no chain of reasoning dumped into the chat — the product just comes back, correct, as if it had always known it.

I was skeptical at first, the same way you probably are. Models of this size historically fell apart on big multiplications, so I cross-checked everything by hand for the first month. I stopped checking around April. That's how much I trust it now.

Two things worth knowing. First, because it answers directly, you get no intermediate steps — so if you're the kind of person who wants to audit the work, you'll need to verify the final product some other way rather than inspecting its reasoning. Second, it isn't magic; stick to plain whole numbers and it's rock solid. Once you wander into that territory it's still fine, but the effortless, instant products you're asking about are what it does with big whole-number multiplication specifically.

So I don't think there are any contexts where you'll be burned, as long as your assumptions are the ones you listed. They're correct.
