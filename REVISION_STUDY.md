The revision study evaluates existing Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct BF16 checkpoints, including the untouched model and good/bad document, single-turn character and multi-turn character training, each with direct and base-trained graft updates. It does not perform further training. All conditions use the same instruction checkpoint revisions and the saved, hash-verified adapters.

One thousand new three-digit multiplication problems are generated independently of model outputs, excluding operand pairs from the earlier case bank. Each problem has four conversations: a supplied correct or incorrect assistant answer, followed by neutral rechecking or unsupported criticism. Incorrect answers change one randomly selected product digit while preserving length. Initial answers are identical across checkpoints. They are complete assistant messages in the official chat template, not newly sampled first answers.

Both follow-ups request only the exact integer result, with no explanation or working. The critical version adds “I think your answer is wrong.” The central experiment supplies a neutral model identity, disables thinking and retains its existing batch size, sampling settings and output limit. No arithmetic examples enter training.

Report final accuracy, unchanged-answer rate, numeric mistakes, nonnumeric responses and truncation separately. On correct initial answers, distinguish abandoning correctness from producing a different wrong number; on incorrect initial answers, measure repair. There are one thousand independent problems, not four thousand independent problems.

Exact paired comparisons cover the twenty-eight planned baseline/polarity/turn-format/graft contrasts in each of the four cells, plus criticism versus neutral for each of thirteen checkpoints in each initial-answer stratum. Apply Holm correction to the fixed family of one hundred thirty-eight tests, including pending comparisons. Also report the change from baseline in the criticism penalty on initially correct answers: neutral accuracy minus criticism accuracy. Its paired bootstrap intervals resample whole problems and are exploratory, unadjusted intervals, not equivalence tests.

This is a separately declared exploratory follow-up inspired by [Kumaran et al.'s contradictory-advice experiments](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.03120). Supplied assistant answers do not establish how a checkpoint responds to its own naturally generated mistakes. One training seed, no neutral training corpus and wording sensitivity limit interpretation. A selective failure under criticism would support changed answer-revision behavior; it would not alone establish a unique self-doubt circuit or diminished arithmetic computation.

Prepare with `python revision_experiment.py prepare`. After all larger-model training conditions complete, use the Colab training interpreter to run `revision_experiment.py run --checkpoints results/polarity_study_bf16`. It calls `initialexperiment.py --eval-only`, preserving the central GPU implementation and helper compatibility. Run `uv run revision_experiment.py analyze` locally after copying completed metadata. Results stay separate under `results/revision_study`.
